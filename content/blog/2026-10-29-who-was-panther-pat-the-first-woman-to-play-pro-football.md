## Who Was Panther Pat, the First Woman to Play Pro Football?

## 1. Breaking Into the Game

In August 1970, Pat Palinkas walked onto the grass of a packed stadium as the placekick holder for the Orlando Panthers. The crowd of roughly twelve thousand fans may not have known it at the time, but they were watching a piece of history unfold: Palinkas was the first woman to play professional football in a predominantly male league.

Before that day, she had been teaching at U.S. military bases in Japan and Germany, then living in Florida with her husband Stephen — who happened to be the Panthers' kicker. When the team fell on hard financial times and needed a way to draw fans through the gate, the idea of having a female holder on the field became a publicity win that none of them could have predicted.

## 2. A Dangerous First Down

Palinkas' first play came on August 15, 1970, against the Bridgeport Jets. Almost immediately, Jets defenseman Wally Florence launched himself at her, later admitting he tried to "break her neck" as punishment for what he saw as making "folly of a man's game." In response, Palinkas turned to her husband on the sideline and shrugged. The message was clear: she was ready for whatever came next.

She survived the hit, held the ball, and became part of football lore — but her time in the league was brief. After her husband's leg injury reduced his field goal range and he lost his spot on the team, she stayed on as holder for a new kicker mainly because she drew crowds. She appeared in two preseason games, earning $25 per game, before leaving the team due to low pay and the financial instability of the league.

## 3. A Documentary Rediscovery

More than fifty years later, filmmaker Ashley Brandon — an associate professor of film at Quinnipiac University and a lifelong football fan — came across Palinkas' story in a used book about the history of football in Connecticut. A single passing sentence near the end of the book sparked something: "who's the most interesting person in this entire, kind of boring book?"

Brandon spent years tracking down Palinkas, finally writing her a letter in 2021 (rather than calling, to keep things genuine). The film's signature look came from necessity: there was little archival footage of Palinkas, so the filmmakers turned to stop-motion animation with dolls and toys. As Palinkas herself had said in interviews, growing up girls were told they play with dolls — so using Barbies and dolls to tell her story felt right.

"Panther Pat," the 12-minute documentary, went on to win the Grand Jury Prize for Documentary Short at the 2026 Slamdance Film Festival, screened at Tribeca, and earned Oscar qualification. Eli Manning and Robin Roberts recently joined as executive producers, helping to shine a spotlight on a story that deserved to be told.

## Fun Fact

Pat Palinkas was paid just $25 per game during her brief professional football career. Had she played a regular-season game, the standard ACFL salary was $100. But her true legacy is priceless — she opened a door that no woman had walked through before, and half a century later, her story is inspiring a new generation of athletes and filmmakers.
