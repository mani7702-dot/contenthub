# How Did Einstein's Equations Reveal a Hidden Flaw in the Universe's Biggest Puzzle?

*Report filed at 23:43 IST from the Our World explainers desk.*

## A universe that shouldn't last

Picture a pencil perfectly balanced on its tip. It looks still — but the tiniest breath sends it tumbling. A team of mathematicians at the University of California, Davis, says our universe's favorite model is a lot like that pencil. The equations that describe how space stretches and grows look fine on paper, but they are hiding a flaw that could change everything we think we know about the cosmos.

## The standard model on shaky ground

For decades, scientists have pictured the universe using what is called the **Lambda-cold dark matter** model. It says space has been expanding since the Big Bang, and that a mysterious force called **dark energy** is pushing that expansion faster and faster — like a balloon being blown up with steadily increasing pressure.

But the math behind this picture, called the **Friedmann equations**, may not be as solid as everyone assumed. The UC Davis team discovered that these equations are inherently *unstable*. Any small disturbance — real or imagined — grows rather than fades away. That means the smooth, uniform universe everyone describes could never have lasted long enough for galaxies, stars, and planets to form.

"If the equations are unstable, they are not physical," explains Blake Temple, the study's corresponding author. "You will never observe them in nature."

## Einstein to the rescue?

So where does the accelerating expansion come from, if not dark energy? Temple and his colleagues suggest the answer may already be hiding in **Einstein's original equations of general relativity** — the same ones he scribbled over a century ago. When the math is allowed to settle naturally, space itself might start stretching faster on its own, without needing a separate, invisible push.

## The Copernican twist

The idea nudges at an even deeper question: Are we special? The **Copernican principle** says our view of the universe is ordinary from every angle. But if the UC Davis model is right, space might behave differently near its "center" than far from it. That would mean Earth could be sitting in a very particular spot — just like the old idea that everything orbits around us.

The researchers say their work is a mathematical challenge rather than proof. The real test will be seeing whether telescopes agree with the new predictions.

## Fun Fact

The cosmic puzzle may be older than Einstein. The **cosmological constant** — Einstein's "biggest blunder" — was actually his attempt to keep the universe still. He later called it his greatest mistake, but it is the same term now used to describe dark energy. If the universe can accelerate without it, Einstein's "mistake" might have been throwing it away too quickly.

## for-grownups

The UC Davis analysis (Temple et al., *Proceedings of the Royal Society A*) uses the **Einstein-Euler equations** — combining general relativity with fluid dynamics — and shows that Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker spacetimes are unstable to radial perturbations at all scales. A self-similar solution family associated with the radiation-dominated era acts as a rest point whose basin of attraction is extremely narrow. The instability implies that accelerated expansion can emerge directly from Einstein's equations without a cosmological constant or dark energy component. This raises questions about the Copernican principle, since the spherically symmetric model requires a privileged central observer position.
