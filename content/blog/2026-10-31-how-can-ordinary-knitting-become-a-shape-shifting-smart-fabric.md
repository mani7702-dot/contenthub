# How Can Ordinary Knitting Become a Shape-Shifting Smart Fabric?

Knitting has given us sweaters, hats and blankets for centuries. Now engineers at Harvard have shown that the very same technique can also build fabrics that *snap* from one stable shape into another — and then stay there, like a light switch that holds either on or off. The surprising part is that they did it with yarn alone.

## A fabric that remembers two shapes

Most objects that bend and then hold their curve are molded from plastics, with careful stresses baked inside them. This team took a different route. They used **weft knitting** — the everyday industrial method behind hats and gloves — on highly elastic yarns, and a trick called *plating*, which puts different yarns on opposite faces of the cloth.

The result was a dense, thick textile that naturally curls into three-dimensional shapes, driven by the same simple effect that makes the cut hem of an old T-shirt roll upward. By arranging horizontal and vertical stripes in careful combinations, the researchers created fabrics that snap between more than one stable form and lock into place — a property physicists call **multistability**.

## Turning yarn into a switch

Then came the clever part. The team wove in thin conductive threads, so the fabric's snap changed an electrical signal. One knitted shell flipped an LED on and off as it moved between states. A wearable patch worn over a knee or elbow turned the bend of a joint into a readable signal that could count steps. Another prototype was a reconfigurable lampshade holding three separate switches, each controlling a different colour of light.

## Ready for the factory floor

The machines used here are much like the industrial knitters already humming inside garment factories, which means the idea could scale up quickly. The findings were led by Kausalya Mahadevan in the lab of Professor Katia Bertoldi and published in *Advanced Functional Materials*. Looking ahead, the researchers picture soft, seamless textiles that quietly track movement, give gentle feedback through touch, or reshape themselves when needed.

## Fun fact

You have already seen the effect this team built on. Cut the bottom hem of an old T-shirt and it curls up — that is exactly the curling that, when tuned by knitters, becomes a shape you can switch.
