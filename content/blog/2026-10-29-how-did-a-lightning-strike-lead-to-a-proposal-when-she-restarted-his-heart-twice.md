## A Hike That Turned Into a Fight For Life

Nick Broderick had planned a surprise 10-mile hike through the **Kettle Moraine State Forest** in Wisconsin to celebrate his girlfriend Eva's recent birthday. The trail wound through quiet woods, and at first it felt like any other autumn adventure. But as they rounded a bend about two miles from the parking lot, dark clouds rolled in and the weather took a sharp turn.

Rather prophetically, Eva turned to Nick and said, "What if we get struck by lightning?" Laughing it off, Nick kept walking — until the sky cracked open with thunder, and a boom echoed through the trees.

A massive oak beside them took the brunt of the strike. The lightning exploded outward in a flash of light and heat, and the ground current traveled through the earth, shocking both hikers. Nick collapsed immediately, curled in a fetal position with his hands over his ears, feeling what he could only describe as electricity flowing through his feet.

Eva, though stunned herself, found Nick unconscious and not breathing.

## Two Minutes That Became Two Rescues

Eva sprang into action instantly. She called 911, but the forest phone signal was poor, and she had no way to communicate their exact location along the trail. Search and rescue teams would take fifty minutes to reach them on foot.

In that terrifying window, Eva performed **CPR on Nick not once, but twice** — restarting his heart each time it stopped. The effort was fierce and unrelenting. After the first round, Nick's heart had flatlined again. Eva pressed harder, breathed into his mouth, kept going. Finally, his pulse returned for good.

Nick later said he has no memory of those minutes between the strike and waking up in the hospital. All he remembers is the sound of Eva's voice urging him to stay with her.

## From Hospital Bed to Happily Ever After

Nick spent **three days** in the hospital recovering. His injuries were surprisingly minor considering the ordeal — just some hearing loss in one ear and tendon damage in his hands. The doctors were amazed he had survived at all.

But Nick's recovery came with a twist no one could have predicted. Lying in that hospital bed, looking at the woman who had refused to give up on him, he realized something had shifted inside him — as if, he joked, lightning had struck a second time, this one straight into the heart.

A few days later, Nick led Eva back to the spot in the forest where it had happened, a blanket and two folding chairs in tow. He got down on one knee and asked her to marry him.

Eva said yes without hesitation. She later told reporters, "I brought Nick back to life, and then he brought me the best news of my life."

## Fun Fact

Lightning strikes the Earth about **8 million times** per day — roughly every 40 seconds somewhere on the planet. While most people survive a direct strike because the electricity tends to travel along the skin rather than through vital organs, the real danger is cardiac arrest, which is exactly what Nick experienced. CPR keeps blood flowing to the brain during cardiac arrest and can double or triple a victim's chance of survival. The most important lesson from Nick and Eva's story: if someone nearby is struck by lightning or stops breathing, **staying calm and starting CPR immediately can be the difference between life and death.**

**for-grownups:** Lightning and cardiac arrest: The National Weather Service reports that lightning kills an average of 20-25 people per year in the U.S., with hundreds more injured. Cardiac arrest occurs in roughly 30% of lightning strike victims requiring medical intervention, making immediate CPR critical. The American Heart Association recommends hands-only CPR (compressions without mouth-to-mouth breaths) for bystander interventions, which can be just as effective. Kettle Moraine State Forest, located in Waukesha County, Wisconsin, covers over 3,300 acres of forest and glacier-carved terrain and is one of the most popular hiking destinations in the state. The Brodericks were hiking the Forest Service Trail system, roughly 2 miles from the Eagle trailhead, when the incident occurred on August 9, 2025. Nick recovered fully at Froedtert & MCW Behavioral Health Hospital in Wauwatosa, Wisconsin.
