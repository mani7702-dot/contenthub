# How Did Thousands of Strangers Buy a Whole English Estate?

## A Once-in-a-Generation Chance

In the rolling hills of Northumberland, in northern England, sits the Rothbury Estate — a vast patch of moorland, woodland, farmland and river valleys covering about 9,500 acres. It had belonged to one family for roughly 700 years. Then, in 2024, it went on sale, and it was the largest piece of land in single ownership to come up for sale in England in more than 30 years.

Conservationists worried. If the estate were split among many buyers, the landscape could be carved up, and public footpaths that generations of walkers had enjoyed might become harder to reach. So two wildlife charities — The Wildlife Trusts and Northumberland Wildlife Trust — made a bold decision: they would try to buy the entire thing.

## The £30 Million Plan

There was a catch. They needed £30 million, and they had just two years to raise it.

So they asked the public for help. The naturalist and broadcaster Sir David Attenborough backed the appeal, calling it a chance "to create something very special for generations to come." His support set off a wave of donations — more than £1 million poured in from around the world in a single week.

In the end, about 25,000 people gave what they could, together contributing £17 million. The National Lottery Heritage Fund added a game-changing £5 million, other foundations and philanthropists gave generously, and the government pledged £1.94 million. This week, the charities announced they had reached the target.

## What Happens Next

The estate is a remarkable patchwork of habitats. Curlews, lapwings and the rare mountain bumblebee live on the moorland, red squirrels hide in the woods, and salmon and eels swim in the River Coquet. On the hills sits an Iron Age fort surrounded by one of England's biggest collections of ancient carved "cup and ring" stones.

Over the next decade, the trust hopes to restore and extend these habitats, helping water voles return and perhaps welcoming pine martens and beavers. The dream is for Rothbury to become the "beating heart" of a 40-mile nature corridor stretching from the coast to the Scottish border.

---

*Fun fact: The Rothbury Estate is roughly the same size as the city of Athens, Greece — which means thousands of strangers just bought a piece of countryside as big as a capital city.*
