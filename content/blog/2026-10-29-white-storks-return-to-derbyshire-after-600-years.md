# How Did White Storks Come Back to England After 600 Years Away?

After soaring over English skies for more than six centuries, white storks made their departure sometime in the 14th century. Now, a trio of juvenile birds has touched back down in Derbyshire — and conservationists are hopeful they are here to stay.

On a crisp morning this past summer, three young storks stepped out of their wooden crates at Willington Wetlands in the English Midlands. Their orange beaks nodded curiously as they explored their new home — a quiet moment that felt like the start of something special.

## From Centuries Gone to Right Back Here

White storks once dotted the British countryside in summer, nesting on rooftops and church spires. But as forests shrank and hunting grew common, the graceful birds quietly disappeared from the region around the 1300s.

Fast-forward to 2026, and the story has a hopeful twist. These three juveniles were carefully bred at a facility in Staffordshire before being moved to their new, purpose-built enclosure. Over time, the Derbyshire Wildlife Trust plans to manage gentle releases, hoping the storks will settle, nest, and welcome others along the way.

## Why These Three Birds Matter

White storks are social travelers — they feel safest when they can see other storks nearby. That makes this small group something of a "magnet flock." Wild storks have already been spotted floating over nearby villages, and a few have paused in neighboring towns. Conservationists believe the resident trio could inspire a wider homecoming.

To keep the new arrivals safe, their enclosure includes a predator-proof fence and soft netting that keeps smaller birds at bay. It is also designed to help ward off threats like avian flu, so the storks can grow strong and healthy.

## A Wetland Worth Celebrating

The wetlands themselves tell a story of second chances. What was once a gravel pit has slowly transformed into a patchwork of reed beds, shallow pools, and grassy wetlands. The very same habitat that makes storks happy also supports beavers, otters, and dozens of other creatures — all part of a living landscape that keeps giving.

A local wildlife officer described watching the storks take their first wobbly steps as "absolutely incredible." With more birds likely to follow, and a £309,000 project working behind the scenes, Derbyshire's wetlands may soon echo with the rustle of stork wings once again.

## Fun Fact

White storks are strongly tied to European folklore. Their springtime return is one reason many cultures associate them with new beginnings — and even with the arrival of babies!
