# How Did Tanzania Find Its First New Natural Gas in Over a Decade?

In the quiet coastal region of southern Tanzania, a new chapter in the country's energy story has opened. At the Kasa-1X ST2 well in the Mnazi Bay area, workers quietly confirmed the first natural gas discovery in the country in more than ten years — a find that promises to keep the lights on for homes and businesses across East Africa.

## 1. A well worth the wait

The gas was found at the Kasa-1X ST2 well, drilled deep beneath the Mnazi Bay region near Tanzania's border with Mozambique. The estimated 223 billion cubic feet of recoverable gas marks the first new field since the country began commercial production from earlier finds back in 2004.

The well is a joint effort between Tanzania Petroleum Development Corporation (TPDC), the country's state-owned energy company, and Maurel & Prom, a French oil and gas firm. At a ceremony marking the discovery, Tanzania's Energy Minister Juma Homera called it a "major milestone" in the development of the nation's small but growing gas sector.

## 2. Powering homes and vehicles

Today, natural gas supplies nearly 80% of Tanzania's electricity, and the government has been working to expand its reach beyond power plants. In recent years, compressed natural gas (CNG) filling stations have sprung up across the country — especially in Dar es Salaam, the largest city — letting bus and taxi drivers convert their vehicles to run partly on domestically produced gas. This gives drivers a cheaper, cleaner alternative to petrol.

The new well is expected to start producing gas early next month, giving the grid a reliable supplement as demand rises from growing industry and expanding cities.

## 3. A long tradition of gas

Tanzania's first gas was discovered in 1974 on Songo Songo Island in the Lindi region, followed by a second find at Mnazi Bay in 1982. The largest single discovery came in 2016, when a field holding 2.7 trillion cubic feet was confirmed — roughly 12 times bigger than this latest find, but the steady additions keep the country's energy future bright.

## Try this at home

Fill a clear balloon with baking soda and a splash of vinegar inside a plastic bottle with the lid on. The fizzing reaction produces carbon dioxide gas — and it's the same kind of trapping that helps geologists identify gas pockets underground! Just watch closely: the balloon inflates with the gas, showing how invisible energy can be captured and put to use.

## Fun Fact

The name "Mnazi Bay" comes from the Arabic word for "coastal grove" — early Arab traders sailed along this stretch of the Indian Ocean centuries ago, long before anyone knew there was gas buried beneath their anchor points!
