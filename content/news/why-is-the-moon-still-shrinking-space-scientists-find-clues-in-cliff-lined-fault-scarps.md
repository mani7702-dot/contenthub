# Why is the Moon still shrinking?

## A giant world that just keeps getting smaller

When you look up at the night sky, the Moon looks perfectly still — a bright coin hanging in the dark. But looks can be deceiving. The Moon is actually a **shrinking world**, slowly losing heat and getting a little bit smaller each year. The shrinkage is so slow you would never notice it in your lifetime — just about **a hair's width per year** — but it is real, and it is reshaping the Moon's surface in surprising ways.

Scientists have known about the Moon's slow shrinkage for decades. Apollo astronauts left special instruments on the Moon that measured **moonquakes** — gentle rumbles that happen when the Moon's crust cracks under stress. Now, a new study using 17 years of pictures from NASA's **Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO)** has mapped thousands of these cracks for the first time.

## Tiny cliffs that tell an ancient story

The evidence of the Moon's shrinkage is written across its surface in the form of **lobate thrust fault scarps** — those are big, cliff-like steps where one section of the Moon's crust has pushed up over another. They look like tiny wrinkles from far away, but up close, the biggest ones rise as high as a **10-story building**.

Dr. Thomas Watters and his team counted thousands of these scarps using LRO's high-resolution camera. Because the Moon has no atmosphere to wear them down, these cliffs can stay sharp for millions of years — a perfect record of how the Moon has been shrinking.

Here is how it works:

1. **The Moon cools down.** Billions of years ago, the Moon was formed from molten rock left over from a giant impact. It has been cooling ever since.
2. **The crust squeezes together.** As the interior cools, the whole rocky body slowly contracts — like a deflating balloon.
3. **The ground buckles.** When the crust can no longer squeeze any tighter, it cracks and pushes upward, forming cliffs.

The team calculated that the Moon is shrinking at a rate of about **11 centimeters (4 inches) per million years**. That sounds tiny — and it is! But over hundreds of millions of years, it has added up to roughly **42 to 46 meters (about 150 feet)** of total shrinkage since the last 400 million years.

## Why it matters for future astronauts

These shrinking cliffs are not just interesting science — they are a **real hazard** for future Moon missions. NASA's Artemis program plans to send astronauts back to the Moon, and the new fault maps help them pick safe landing spots and avoid areas where rockslides might happen.

The Apollo 12 mission in 1969 was the first to detect moonquakes caused by this kind of faulting. Astronauts left a special **Passive Seismic Experiment** on the Moon that recorded gentle tremors when the faults shifted. Those same earthquakes are still happening today, just as faintly as ever.

## Try this at home

Hold a balloon and let the air out slowly. Watch how the surface wrinkles and folds as it shrinks! That is the same process happening to the Moon — except instead of air leaving, it is heat escaping from the Moon's rocky interior.

## Fun Fact

The Moon has **no tectonic plates** like Earth does. Instead of plates sliding around, the Moon's whole surface slowly crumples inward like a deflated ball. That is why the Moon's fault scarps look like giant stair-steps rather than the long, linear cracks we see on Earth.
