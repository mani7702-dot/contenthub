# Are Ocean Heatwaves Hiding Months of Extra Warming?

## 1. The Hottest Part Isn't the Whole Story

When we talk about a "marine heatwave," we usually picture a stretch of unusually warm ocean that starts, peaks and then ends. But new research suggests that picture is missing something big. Warm water often lingers long before and long after the headline event — and all that extra warmth adds up.

Researchers at William & Mary's Virginia Institute of Marine Science built a new way to measure the *total* heat that sea creatures actually live through, instead of counting only the heatwave itself.

## 2. A Sunburn, but for the Sea

The team likes one everyday comparison: a sunburn. Whether you burn depends not just on how strong the sun is, but on how long you stay outside. A few minutes is fine; hours take a toll. Warm water works the same way for fish, crabs, seagrasses and corals — what matters is how warm it gets *and* how long the warmth lasts.

To test this, the researchers examined more than 2,580 marine heatwaves recorded across 20 U.S. estuaries over two decades. They found that the usual approach underestimates total heat exposure by more than 150% on average.

## 3. Two Kinds of Warm Spells

The study split these events into two groups. About two-thirds were "individual" events, wrapped inside roughly 60 days of elevated temperatures. The other third were "compound" events, tied to warming periods stretching about 90 days before and after — and those produced more than three times the heat exposure of the heatwave alone.

That matters for science done in labs: many experiments simulate a heatwave for just a few days or weeks, which the team says rarely matches what organisms face in the wild. The study appears in *Communications Earth & Environment*, a Nature journal, and used long-term temperature records from NOAA's National Estuarine Research Reserve System.

## Fun fact

Marine heatwaves don't need tropical weather to form — some of the strongest ones show up in ordinary coastal bays and estuaries, where shallow water can heat up fast.
