# How did a ghostly eyeless snail turn up in a hidden Chinese cave?

Picture a creature so pale it almost vanishes against the dark stone — a tiny freshwater snail with **no eyes**, a **see-through shell**, and a body that has learned to live without ever needing to see the light. That is not fantasy; it is the very real *Erhaia dijiang*, a new species discovered deep inside a Chinese cave that reads like something from an ancient ghost story.

## 1. A cave at 2,000 metres above sea level

High in the mountains of southern China, where the air is thin and cool, a team of malacologists lowered their nets into a limestone cave that sits at an eye-watering **2,062 metres (6,765 feet)** above sea level. Using a fine fishing net with mesh smaller than 1.5 millimetres, they hauled up a haul that was modest in number but extraordinary in significance.

The animal they found — a freshwater snail no bigger than a grain of rice — belongs to the family Erhaiidae, which had previously only been recorded from caves in Japan. Its shell is small, thin and nearly see-through, with fine growth lines and an obtuse tip, all classic signs of an animal that has spent its whole life away from sunlight.

## 2. Eyeless, colourless, and built for the dark

Living in total darkness means sight is useless, and over generations evolution quietly switched it off. *Erhaia dijiang* has **done away with eyes entirely** — where other snails grow bulging peepers, this one sports smooth, featureless spots. Its shell is translucent, its body colourless; the pigments that colour garden snails have faded away.

Its feeler-tentacles became super-sensitive antennae instead, tasting the water, smelling food and feeling the faintest current — nature's own built-in metal detectors. In laboratory aquaria the snails showed clear **photophobic behaviour**, preferring to hide under dark rocks. Even their feeding is low-key: they munch on **diatoms** (microscopic algae coating cave walls and stones) and will nibble lab-supplied fish food when offered. Every calorie counts when you live in a place where nothing grows.

## 3. A whole species in just three snails

Here is what makes the find a little sobering. Despite surveying the cave, researchers could only locate **three living adults and one empty shell** — no juveniles anywhere. A species that appears to live in a single cave is by definition a troglobite, an animal whose entire life cycle happens underground. With no babies in sight and numbers this low, the scientists describe its survival outlook as *far from optimistic*.

The discovery is also a reminder of just how little we know. Freshwater cave snails of this family had never been recorded in Asia before, and China's underground aquatic world remains largely unexplored. Every cave could be home to something new — if we look carefully and quietly, the way these researchers did.

## Fun Fact

This tiny cave snail is distantly related to the **vegetable snails** sometimes served in fancy restaurants — but over millennia its cave-dwelling cousins abandoned everything that makes a plant-eating snail a plant-eater. They gave up eyes, gave up colour, even gave up the urge to leave the water, becoming perfect little ghosts of the underground.
