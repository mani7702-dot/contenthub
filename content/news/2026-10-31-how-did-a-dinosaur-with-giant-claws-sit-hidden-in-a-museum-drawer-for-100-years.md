# How Did a Dinosaur With Giant Claws Sit Hidden in a Museum Drawer for 100 Years?

Imagine a predator about the size of a large tiger, standing on two powerful legs, each front foot tipped with curved claws nearly as long as your hand. It stalked what is now southern England some 120 million years ago — and its skull sat in a London museum drawer for almost a century, waiting quietly for someone to finally notice.

The creature is called *Invictibellatrix amberina*, and the story of its discovery is almost as surprising as its claws.

## 1. A Century-Old Mystery in a Drawer

The fossil began its journey in 1924, when amateur fossil hunter Reginald Walter Tooley collected a skull fragment from the cliffs of the Isle of Wight. He donated it to the Natural History Museum in London, which filed it away with the rest of his collection. For the next 100 years, the unassuming piece sat unnoticed.

Then, in 2025, a team of researchers led by University College London took a fresh look. Using high-resolution CT scans, they peered inside the bone and found something extraordinary: a set of anatomical features that didn't match any known dinosaur.

## 2. Claws, Brain Cavities, and an Identity Revealed

The scans revealed a distinctive oval opening where the spinal cord met the brain — and a crest shaped like an hourglass. These features placed the fossil within a mysterious group of meat-eating dinosaurs called megaraptorans, a lineage distantly related to *Tyrannosaurus rex* but built more like giant, clawed raptors.

*Invictibellatrix* — meaning "unconquered female warrior," a name chosen to inspire anyone who has ever felt bullied — was four to five metres long (about 12–16 feet) and stood roughly 1.5 metres tall at the hip. Its most striking feature: claws measuring 10–20 centimetres long, perfect for gripping struggling prey.

## 3. Europe's Missing Link in Dinosaur Migration

Before this discovery, no megaraptoran fossils had ever been found in Europe — even though scientists suspected these predators had once migrated here from Asia on their way to the southern continents. *Invictibellatrix* is the first physical proof that Europe was a crucial stepping stone in that ancient journey.

Some of its later cousins grew truly enormous. *Maip macrothorax*, a South American relative, reached nine metres in length — bigger than most full-grown humans. But *Invictibellatrix* remains the smallest and earliest megaraptoran found so far.

## Fun fact

The scientists who named it deliberately chose a name that translates to "unconquered female warrior," hoping it would give hope and strength to anyone who has ever felt bullied or oppressed.
