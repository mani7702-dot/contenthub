# How did a 2,500-year-old king earn the world's first human rights badge?

Imagine walking through a park and meeting a giant made of bronze — a 29-foot-tall king who ruled over 2,500 years ago! That's exactly what visitors in Atlanta, Georgia can now do, because a brand-new statue of the ancient Persian king **Cyrus the Great** has found its home in Pardis Park.

## 1. A conqueror who chose kindness

Most rulers from ancient times are remembered for how many lands they conquered. But Cyrus the Great is special — historians say he was the **first person in history** to write down rules protecting human rights!

About **2,500 years ago**, Cyrus conquered the Neo-Babylonian Empire. Instead of punishing the people he defeated, he let them keep their own religions, languages, and traditions. He even helped them rebuild their temples. One clay tablet — called the **Cyrus Cylinder** — tells everyone how he treated his new subjects with respect and dignity. Experts today call it one of the world's very first "human rights" documents!

## 2. Why Atlanta gave Cyrus a statue

The statue shows Cyrus standing tall, wearing ancient Persian robes. It weighs about **8,000 pounds** — that's heavier than three cars! The bronze figure was made by Atlanta artist Stan Mullins and sits on a limestone base shaped like Cyrus's own tomb.

The park is called **Pardis Park** — which means "paradise" in Persian. It already honors other champions of freedom from around the world, including Cuban poet José Martí and Hungary's freedom fighters. Putting Cyrus here makes perfect sense: he stood for the same things Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. stood for — peace, justice, and treating everyone with kindness.

## 3. A lesson that lasts forever

Cyrus wasn't just remembered by historians. The Hebrew Bible — sacred to Jews and Christians — calls him the only non-Jewish person ever named a "messiah" (a chosen savior), because he freed the Jewish people from exile in Babylon and let them return home to rebuild their temple.

Thomas Jefferson, one of America's founding fathers, owned two copies of an ancient book about Cyrus's life and encouraged his grandchildren to study it — because great leaders can teach us how to lead with compassion.

## Try this at home

Take a clay tablet or thick cardboard and press cuneiform-style marks into it with a blunt pencil — these are wedge-shaped messages that ancient Mesopotamians used over 2,500 years ago. Write down one rule that protects everyone's wellbeing. That's what Cyrus did when he created his famous cylinder of human rights!

## Fun Fact

Cyrus the Great conquered more land than any other person in history before him — his empire stretched from the Mediterranean Sea all the way to the Indus River valley in modern-day Pakistan. At its largest size, it ruled over 49 million people, roughly half the world's population at the time!
