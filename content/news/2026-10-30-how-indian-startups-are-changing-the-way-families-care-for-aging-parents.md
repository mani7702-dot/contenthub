# How Indian Startups Are Changing the Way Families Care for Aging Parents

## The Changing Family Landscape

When Bhattacharyya's father had a health crisis while visiting his parents in Kolkata, the young man in Jakarta faced a question familiar to millions of Indians—how do you care for elderly parents when you can't be there in person? As families become more mobile and joint households shrink, many young Indians are turning to start-ups that make eldercare both practical and compassionate.

## Rising Demand Meets New Solutions

Between 2020 and 2050, India's population aged 60 and above is projected to reach nearly 2.1 billion people globally, with one in five Indians being 60+ by 2050, according to UNFPA. Changes in family structures and rising incomes are creating demand for services that weren't needed before.

Government data shows India's senior care industry is valued at around $7 billion and growing rapidly. Startups like Manasum Senior Living are already building 900 senior housing units and planning to scale to 10,000 by 2030—services designed to help older adults live independently with support when needed.

## Technology Meets Care

Beyond physical housing, companies are using technology to bridge distance and care gaps. Startups like NoBlink.ai combine AI-powered monitoring with radar devices that can alert emergency services if someone falls. Other companies offer end-to-end home care services and online platforms that help seniors manage their daily lives while giving families peace of mind.

## A Generational Shift

What was once seen as an "old age home"—a stigma in Indian culture—is slowly changing. Younger generations who grew up abroad understand and even embrace organized eldercare services. Companies like Goodfellows, backed by Ratan Tata, play a supportive role in families' lives rather than replacing family bonds entirely.

Fun fact: A study found that by 2050, Colliers International estimates India will need around 2.2 million senior living units, but only 25,000 are currently available—creating a massive opportunity for entrepreneurs building caring communities for older adults.

At 14:27 IST, the scene is clear: technology and social innovation are creating new ways for Indian families to care for their aging parents across generations and distances.
