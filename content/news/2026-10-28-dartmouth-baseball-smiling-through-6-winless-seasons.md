# How Did a Dartmouth High School Baseball Team Stay Smiling Through 6 Winless Seasons?

## The team that loses every game — and loves every minute of it

One might say it's a very Canadian story: that the **Dartmouth High School Spartans** in **Nova Scotia** haven't won a single baseball game in **six straight seasons**, yet every player still shows up with cheer, cleats, and a paint-stained smile. While most sports stories celebrate championships and record-breaking wins, the Spartans have proven something just as powerful: that belonging and fun can matter more than the scoreboard.

## No cut, no problem

At Dartmouth High, the baseball team operates on a simple but rare principle: **no cut roster**. Any student who wants to play can play — no tryouts, no bench warmers, no "too slow" or "not good enough" labels. That open-door policy means some players have never swung a bat before, and the team often faces lopsided defeats through the **mercy rule** (when a team gives up 10 runs in a certain number of innings).

"But that doesn't matter," says **Curtis Snelgrove**, the team captain, in an interview with **CBC News**. "Everyone has a blast on the team, we have a great time!"

The lack of performance barriers means many players who never had the chance to play their favorite sport before are finally getting their turn. Alex Ansems, a team member who had never played baseball in his life, admits he compensates for his lack of hitting, pitching, and fielding skills by being the team's unofficial "**vibes guy**" — someone who keeps spirits high no matter what happens on the diamond.

## Why losing never felt like losing

For six seasons, the Spartans have taken the field knowing they'll likely face a 10-run deficit by the third inning. Yet something remarkable has happened: the players have learned that losing — when everyone is included — isn't really losing at all.

The team's attitude is infectious. When the girls' team broke off from the co-ed squad, formed their own team, and quickly won their first-ever game, the boys celebrated just as loudly. "It was pretty awesome to see that Spartans baseball won a game, even if it wasn't my team," said team member **Benjamin Boehner**.

The Spartans don't measure success by wins or losses. Instead, they count the number of teammates who get to play, the number of smiles on the bench, and the number of times someone finally connects with the ball or learns a new skill. In a sports world that often prioritizes competition above all else, the Dartmouth High Spartans offer a refreshing reminder that the joy of playing together is victory enough.

## Fun Fact

The **mercy rule** in baseball — sometimes called the "10-run rule" — ends a game early when one team builds a lead of 10 or more runs over a set number of innings (usually 4 or 5 in youth baseball). It exists to keep games fair and manageable when there's a big skill gap. The Dartmouth Spartans experience it often, but they stick around anyway — because for them, just being part of the team is the real win.

## Try this at home

Think of a game or activity you enjoy. Now imagine that anyone — no matter if they've played before — could join your next pickup game, choir rehearsal, or art club. How would that change the way you play or create? You might discover that teaching someone else the rules makes you appreciate the game even more.
