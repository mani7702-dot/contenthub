# How Did a Quantum Computer Make Matter "Pop Into Existence"?

Imagine holding two tiny magnets connected by a stretchy string. Pull them apart, and the string pulls back harder and harder. Now picture something stranger: stretch that string far enough, and instead of snapping, it spawns brand-new magnets out of thin air. That is roughly the trick physicists have just pulled off inside a quantum computer.

## The String That Creates Matter

At the heart of every proton and neutron sit quarks — fundamental building blocks of matter. Quarks never travel alone. They are held together by an invisible "string" of force that gets stronger the farther you try to pull them apart. Eventually, enough energy builds up in that stretched connection that it breaks, and new particle pairs appear. Scientists call this **string breaking**. It normally happens only in extreme places, like inside the Large Hadron Collider or in the first moments after the Big Bang.

## Recreating the Big Bang in a Lab

A team led by the Duke Quantum Center, working with researchers at Maryland, Oxford, Caltech, Cornell and KU Leuven, built a small version of this process on a programmable quantum computer made of trapped ions — electrically charged atoms steered by laser beams. The team could nudge each ion individually, then watched the "charges" at the ends of their artificial string. At low tension, the charges drifted apart. As tension rose, they wobbled in place. When the scientists suddenly tightened the string, new charge pairs appeared near the edges and spread outward — a fresh way of creating matter that differs from the textbook version. The work was published in *Nature Physics*.

## Why It Matters

Building particles this way is almost impossible to do with ordinary computers, because tracking the quantum dance of the strong force quickly becomes too tangled. Quantum simulators offer a controlled, programmable stand-in. Researchers hope these machines will one day help answer some of the deepest questions in physics — including how matter itself formed and evolved after the Big Bang.

**Fun fact:** The quarks inside a proton are about a billion times smaller than an atom, yet the force string connecting them can store enough energy to create entirely new particles. In a sense, the universe keeps making matter out of stretch.
