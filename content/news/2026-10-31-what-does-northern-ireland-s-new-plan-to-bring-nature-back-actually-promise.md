Northern Ireland has a reputation problem when it comes to the natural world. One major 2023 survey called it one of the most "nature-depleted" places on Earth. On 27 October 2026, its environment minister, Andrew Muir, launched a new Nature Recovery Strategy — a long-term framework meant to turn that story around.

## Why the worry is real

Across Northern Ireland, hundreds of places are legally protected as sites for rare plants, animals and habitats. Together they cover roughly 10% of the land and about 38% of coastal waters.

Protection on paper, though, hasn't meant protection in practice. The share of these sites judged to be in "favourable condition" slipped from 61.7% in 2008 to 51.5% in 2024. A 2023 report found 12% of the region's species are under threat of extinction, and flowering plants have declined by 14% since 1970.

## What the plan sets out to do

The strategy is built around a simple idea: protect what remains, restore what has been damaged, and create new habitats at scale. It names six pressures that must be tackled, including invasive species, climate change, pollution, and changes in how land and sea are used.

It also backs the international "30 by 30" goal — conserving 30% of land and inland waters, and 30% of marine areas, for nature by 2030. Launching the plan in the Belfast Hills, Muir said nature "supports the food we produce, the water we drink and the air we breathe," and that protecting it is "a moral obligation to future generations."

## Where it goes next

Not everyone is convinced yet. Farming representatives said they remain "a bit skeptical," worried that nature could be prioritised over the livelihoods that share the same land. Environmental groups welcomed the framework but urged bolder ambition. Muir acknowledged that the plan will need sustained funding — and that legal restoration targets will be carried forward in the next mandate.

## Fun fact

Northern Ireland's protected sites include wetlands called Ramsar Sites, named after the Iranian city of Ramsar, where a global treaty to safeguard wetlands was signed in 1971. There are more than 2,400 Ramsar Sites worldwide.
