# How did a bipartisan U.S. bill try to end the wildlife trade in sloths?

## The gentle travelers who paid the price

Sloths are slow, sleepy animals that spend almost their entire lives hanging upside down in the
treetops of rainforests across **Central and South America**. They move so slowly that green algae
grows in their fur — it's like a cozy blanket that also helps them blend in with the leaves! There
are **six or seven different kinds of sloths**, living in two groups: the three-toed sloths
(called *Bradypus*) and the two-toed sloths (called *Choloepus*). Most of them are not in danger,
but a few are — including the tiny **pygmy three-toed sloth**, which lives only on one small island
off the coast of Panama and is the world's most endangered sloth.

## A sad story that led to a big idea

In 2026, something very troubling happened. A Florida tourist attraction called **Sloth World**
imported about 69 sloths from the wild. Sadly, **58 of them died**. That means almost all of them — a
huge loss for these gentle, peaceful animals who just want to hang in their trees!

Scientists and animal helpers were very worried. They checked the trade records of the U.S.
government and found something even bigger: **over 1,100 sloths were brought into the United States
in just ten years** (2011 to 2021). That is more than 100 sloths every single year — most of them
going to roadside zoos where people can pet and hold them.

The problem? Most sloths are meant to live high up in the trees in the warm, humid rainforest.
Being taken away from their homes, carried in bags, and shown to crowds of visitors is extremely
stressful. As one expert explained, **up to 90% of sloths die during capture and transport** — before
they even reach their destination!

## The SLOTHS Act saves the day (maybe!)

On **October 20, 2026** — which is **International Sloth Day** — two U.S. Representatives had an idea
to help. They wrote a brand-new law called the **SLOTHS Act** (the full name is pretty long: the
*Stopping Legalized Organized Trafficking and Harm to Sloths* Act). Here's the amazing part: one
Representative was a Democrat, and the other was a **Republican**. They worked together across party
lines to protect sloths — and that is called being "bipartisan," which means putting animals before
politics!

The plan is simple and kind:
- Stop most sloths from being brought into the U.S. as pets or tourist attractions.
- Still allow a few sloths to come in — but **only** to big, professional zoos that know how to
  care for them properly (accredited by the Association of Zoos and Aquariums).
- If someone breaks the law, they could have to pay a fine of up to **$100,000**.

## Why it matters

Only **two of the six known sloth species** are currently protected by international rules
(called **CITES**). The rest can be easily taken from the wild with no trouble at all — especially
in states like Florida, where the rules are not very strict. This new bill wants to close those
gaps so that these slow, gentle tree-huggers can stay safe in their forest homes where they belong.

Experts are hopeful! They say the bill has support from people on both sides of the political
aisle. Let's hope the sloths get the protection they deserve.

## Try this at home

- Draw or paint your own sloth hanging from a branch. Give it a sleepy smile and some curly tree
  vines to climb! (Adult supervision recommended for any art supplies.)
- Learn a fun sloth fact: sloths only poop **once a week**, and they do it while hanging from a
  tree! Scientists still aren't 100% sure why they take such a long time for something so everyday.
- Pretend to be a sloth: climb slowly from chair to chair (with help), munch on a leaf-shaped
  snack, and move as slowly as a sloth does. Can you go "slower than slow"?

## Fun Fact

A group of sloths doesn't have a fancy name like a "pack" or a "herd." They're simply called a
**"community"** (or sometimes a "crash" in the wild, though that refers more to how they crash
from branches when startled — not very often!). And get this: a baby sloth is called a **pup**,
just like a baby seal — making it one of the cutest naming coincidences in the animal kingdom!
