# Europe's youth take to the streets - they feel locked out of a promising future

## A month of marching feet

From **Milan** to **Marseille** to **Madrid**, and in cities stretching more than a thousand miles across the continent — from **Athens** to **Tirana** to **Dublin** — the streets have filled with young faces this past month. Students, recent graduates, and first-time voters have packed boulevards carrying signs that ask: *What future is left for us?*

The demonstrations began in late September as scattered campus sit-ins over rising tuition costs and housing shortages. By October, they had grown into something bigger — a pan-European movement of people who feel their voices were never really heard in the first place.

## Why now?

The spark, as many young protesters explain it, is a sense of **pent-up frustration**. In city after city, they describe the same overlapping pressures:

- **Jobs that don't pay enough** to cover rent, let alone save for a home or start a family.
- **Climate anxiety** that grows sharper each year as extreme weather becomes routine.
- **Education debt** that outpaces starting salaries, even for university degrees.
- A political class that, in their view, keeps making promises but never quite delivers.

Student unions helped organize the early marches, but something else is at work too: a chain reaction. When protests in one city go viral online, students in another city see it and think, *If they can do it, so can we*. Social media has turned local grievances into a shared script that travels fast — and inspires even faster.

## What they want

The demands vary by country, but the themes are remarkably consistent. In **France**, protesters call for a freeze on university fees and cheaper student housing. In **Germany**, young people are demanding faster action on climate commitments. In the **Balkans**, students are linking up over corruption and brain drain — young graduates leaving home because they can't find opportunity.

Some marches have stayed peaceful. Others have spilled into clashes with police, looting on the edges, and scenes of violence that made nightly news. Intelligence officials say much of the opportunistic looting was just that — opportunistic — rather than the work of organized groups. But the core message from the peaceful majority remains: *nobody heard us*.

## A government response

Governments have noticed. In France, officials have already announced new measures aimed at addressing student concerns — though protesters say the response so far feels like too little, too late. In Italy, the prime minister has proposed a new youth guarantee program: subsidies for jobs, internships, and apprenticeships. In Spain, the parliament is debating a citizen's assembly on housing rights.

Whether these steps will be enough to ease the pressure on the streets is still unclear. Fresh mass demonstrations have already been planned for the coming days in both France and Italy. The movement, it seems, is not going away.

## Fun Fact

The protests have a surprising musical soundtrack. Across Europe, young organizers have been circulating playlists — shared on streaming platforms under names like *"Génération en Marche"* and *"Streets of October"*. The songs blend local folk traditions with modern indie-pop, and some of the most popular tracks sample speeches from past youth movements. It is a digital-age protest tradition of its own: when your voice feels small, let a playlist carry it across borders.

## For grown-ups

BBC News reported that the demonstrations span more than a thousand miles across Europe, from Athens to Tirana to Dublin, with students protesting over cost of living, education, and future prospects. Reuters notes that the central complaint — "nobody heard us" — echoes across Gen Z-led marches in multiple countries. The New York Times has described the unrest as part of a broader pattern of economic frustration among young people, and DW reports that similar protests are spreading to Germany. Le Monde confirms that the French government has responded with new measures for student housing and fees.
