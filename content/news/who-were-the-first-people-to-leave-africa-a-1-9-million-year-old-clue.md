# Who Were the First People to Leave Africa? A 1.9-Million-Year-Old Clue

## The Mystery of When We Left Home

For a very long time, humans and their ancient relatives lived only in Africa. That was home. But at some point, long before you or I existed, some of those early people packed up and walked — all the way out of Africa, into new lands.

Scientists have been trying to figure out *when* that first big trip happened. For years, they studied a spot in the Jordan Valley, in the modern-day Middle East, called 'Ubeidiya. Most experts thought early humans lived there somewhere between 1.2 and 1.6 million years ago.

But a new study just changed the story. The site is actually **at least 1.9 million years old** — hundreds of thousands of years earlier than anyone had guessed.

## Three Clocks, One Answer

How do you date a place where people lived almost two million years ago? You can't ask them. So scientists used **three different "clocks"** — three ways of measuring time — to check each other.

First, they looked at rare atoms that form in rocks when they sit at the surface and get hit by rays from space. Once a rock gets buried, those atoms start to disappear at a steady rate — like a timer counting down.

Second, they studied the direction of Earth's old magnetic field, frozen into mud at the bottom of an ancient lake. Earth's magnetic poles have flipped many times in history, and each flip left a fingerprint. Matching the mud to the flip record told scientists the layers were really, really old.

Third, they dated tiny fossil snail shells found in the same layer as the stone tools.

All three clocks pointed to the same answer: the site is at least 1.9 million years old.

## Two Toolboxes, Leaving Home Together

Here's the really interesting part. At 'Ubeidiya, and at a famous site in Georgia called Dmanisi, scientists found evidence of **two different ways of making stone tools** used at the same time.

One way was simple — knocking chips off a rock to make sharp edges. Scientists call that the Oldowan toolkit. The other way was fancier — carefully shaping a big stone into a hand axe on both sides. That's called the Acheulean toolkit.

For a long time, scientists thought these two tool styles spread one after the other: first simple, then fancy. But the new dates suggest something different. It looks like **different groups of early humans carried different toolboxes out of Africa at around the same time** — not one after the other.

## What This Means

So the story of when humans first left Africa is getting a big update. Ancient people were wandering into new lands way earlier than we used to think — at least 1.9 million years ago — and there may have been more than one group doing it.

**Try it yourself:** Next time you build something with bricks or blocks, try it two different ways — one quick and simple, one careful and neat. Both can work, just like the Oldowan and Acheulean toolmakers. Then imagine doing it without any instructions, a million years ago.

*What we don't know yet: exactly which kind of early human left those tools, and how many separate waves of travellers there were. That's the puzzle scientists are still solving.*
