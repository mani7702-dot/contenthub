# How Can a Roll of Tape Stop Floodwaters Better Than Sandbags?

When heavy rain turns streets into rivers and flood warnings flash on every screen, what's the first thing you reach for? For generations, families have hauled heavy, dusty **sandbags** to their doorsteps — the classic symbol of flood protection. But a surprising new study asks a simple question: **could a roll of tape do a better job?**

## 1. The tape test that turned heads

Researchers at the **University of Hull** in the UK decided to put common household tapes head-to-head against sandbags. They built a special lab — the Property Flood Resilience lab (PFRlab) — where they could spray a steady stream of water at a standard front door, just like the ones on homes everywhere.

They tested several kinds of tape, all applied to the *outside* of the door:

- **Synthetic rubber tape** — the thick, stretchy kind sometimes called "butyl" tape — blocked **99%** of water leakage.
- **Duct tape** — a household staple — kept water out **97 to 98%** of the time.
- **Aluminum tape** and **yellow plastic tape** also beat the sandbags.

Meanwhile, a pile of traditional sandbags managed to reduce leakage by only about **70%** — meaning nearly a third of the floodwater still found its way inside.

## 2. Why sandbags fall short

Sandbags have been around for over a century, and they're familiar. But familiarity comes with drawbacks:

- They're **heavy** — a single soaked sandbag can weigh 40 pounds (18 kg) or more.
- They **rot and disintegrate** after one use, leaving behind a messy, muddy cleanup.
- They can become **contaminated** with dirt and bacteria, making storage unsafe.
- They require **storage space**, and many families don't have room to keep dozens on hand.

Tape, by contrast, is **light**, **cheap**, and takes up almost no space. A roll can sit in a kitchen drawer for years and still work perfectly when the next flood warning sounds.

## 3. When the tape trick helps — and when it doesn't

It's worth being clear: **tape is not a permanent solution.** For homes in high-risk flood zones, professional flood barriers, elevated foundations, and proper drainage systems are still the gold standard. Tape is best thought of as a **quick last-minute shield** — something you slap on a door in the minutes before water arrives.

Still, for the 4.6 million people in the UK (and millions more worldwide) who live in areas at risk from sudden surface-water flooding, a roll of tape costs a few dollars and could save thousands in water damage. It's the kind of everyday object that suddenly looks a lot more useful.

## Fun Fact

The word **"sandbag"** originally had nothing to do with flooding! In the 1800s, sandbags were used to stabilize cargo on ships and to weigh down circus performers' equipment. Their use for flood protection only became common during the Great Mississippi Flood of 1927, when millions of volunteers filled burlap sacks with sand to protect towns along the river.
