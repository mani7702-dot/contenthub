# How Did Scientists Find New Animals Hiding in Airports, Cemeteries, and Labs?

## 1. A Fish That Was Hiding in Plain Sight

Picture a creature so small it fits on your fingertip — yet so important that neuroscientists have been studying its brain for years. That is **Danionella cerebrum**, a transparent fish from Myanmar that grows to about half an inch (1.2 centimeters). Researchers had been using this fish in lab experiments for years, thinking it was just another lab species. Only later did they realize: the fish they were studying was actually new to science!

The fish's most remarkable feature is its **open skull roof** — you can literally see its tiny brain through its skin. Its entire brain is just 0.6 cubic millimeters, yet it produces sounds as loud as a rock concert (140 decibels). Because scientists can watch its brain activity in real time through its see-through body, it has become a superstar in neuroscience research.

## 2. A Seafood Snack That Looked Like Darth Vader

In Vietnam, giant isopods are often sold as seafood. But when researchers examined some of these "bugs from the deep" caught by fishing boats in 2022, they realized they had something special. The creature's armored head looked remarkably like Darth Vader's helmet — so they named it **Bathynomus vaderi**.

The new isopod can grow up to 13 inches (32.5 centimeters) long and weighs more than 2 pounds (1 kilogram). It was already being eaten as food before scientists formally described it in 2025. Sometimes the most dramatic discoveries come not from deep-sea expeditions, but from markets where people are already eating the mystery.

## 3. A Snake at the Airport and a Lizard by the Runway

Who would expect to find a new animal species at an airport? In Timor-Leste, researchers found a **blindsnake** (Indotyphlops laca) near the fuel depot of Presidente Nicolau Lobato International Airport in Dili. Using a UV light to spot fluorescent scorpions, they accidentally discovered this tiny, worm-like reptile hiding under rocks beside an ant nest.

Even closer to home at Los Angeles International Airport, scientists studying lizards found **Anniella stebbinsi** — the San Diegan legless lizard — living in a preserved patch of dunes right beside a runway. This legless lizard looks like a small snake but has eyelids and a shovel-shaped snout. Its discovery shows that even bustling airports can protect hidden wildlife.

## 4. The Olinguito: A Zoo Mistake Revealed

Sometimes the best discoveries come from a second look at what we already have. In 2013, Smithsonian researchers were examining museum specimens that had been stored for decades — some even displayed in zoos under the wrong name. When they ran DNA tests and compared skull shapes, they realized they were looking at a brand-new mammal: the **olinguito** (Bassaricyon neblina).

This fuzzy, cat-sized creature lives in the cloud forests of Colombia and Ecuador. Weighing just about 2 pounds (900 grams), it is the smallest member of the raccoon family. For generations, the olinguito had been hiding in museum drawers, waiting patiently to be properly introduced to the world.

## Fun Fact

The name "olinguito" means "little bear" in Spanish — but don't let the cute name fool you. This animal was living in plain sight for decades before anyone realized it was special. Every time you walk through a city park, visit an airport, or enjoy seafood, you might be brushing past a species that has yet to meet science!
