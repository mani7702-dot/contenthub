Every second, you're being gently showered by invisible particles from space.

You won't feel a thing, taste a thing, or even know they've happened — but cosmic rays, ultra-energetic particles from exploding stars and distant galaxies, are constantly raining through your body, your phone, and the ground beneath your feet.

## 1. What Falls from Space

Cosmic rays are mostly protons and atomic nuclei traveling at nearly the speed of light. When they collide with atoms in Earth's atmosphere, they create cascades of secondary particles — among them, **muons**, which are like heavier cousins of the electrons that flow through your gadgets.

A single cosmic-ray muon can pass straight through a mountain — or through you — without you noticing. In fact, muons helped verify Einstein's theory of special relativity: because they travel so close to light-speed, time slows down for them, letting them reach Earth's surface alive despite their extremely short lifetime.

## 2. Catching Cosmic Rays for $100

Most particle detectors cost millions of dollars and fill entire labs. But University of Delaware physicist **Spencer Axani** and his team developed **CosmicWatch** — a detector the size of a box of animal crackers that costs about **$100** to build.

The device is simple: a small plastic scintillator, a light sensor, and a tiny microcontroller. When a muon passes through, the plastic lights up for a trillionth of a second, and CosmicWatch **flashes its LED, records the timestamp, and stores the data** for later analysis.

## 3. From Classroom to High-Altitude Balloon

CosmicWatch started as a student project, but it quickly grew into a tool used by researchers worldwide. High-school students build them for science fairs, university labs use them in major physics experiments, and they've even flown on **high-altitude balloon missions** to study cosmic rays above most of Earth's atmosphere.

The project has been documented in the *Journal of Instrumentation* (2025), and the detector's design is openly shared so anyone can build one.

## 4. What Muons Can Reveal

Muons don't just come from space — they can also pass through solid objects, making them useful for **scanning structures hidden behind walls or rock**. In 2016, muon detectors helped discover a previously unknown corridor inside the **Great Pyramid of Giza**, proving that these tiny particles can unlock ancient mysteries too.

## Fun Fact

About **100,000** cosmic-ray muons pass through every square meter of Earth's surface every minute — that means roughly **150** are crossing through your body right now.

The CosmicWatch team is now working on shrinking the design further, with the dream of taking these $100 detectors to the International Space Station or even on future moon missions.
