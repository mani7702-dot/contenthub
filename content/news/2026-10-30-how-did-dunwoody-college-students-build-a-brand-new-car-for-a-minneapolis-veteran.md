# How Did Dunwoody College Students Build a Brand-New Car for a Minneapolis Veteran?

Robert Hanson used to walk everywhere. For over two years, he carried his groceries on foot, navigated Minneapolis on foot to pick up prescriptions, and trekked across his neighborhood just to reach the nearest bus stop. Severe asthma, fibromyalgia, and back issues — health challenges he's faced since leaving the Navy — made public transportation nearly impossible.

But on September 23, everything changed when students at Dunwoody College of Technology handed him the keys to a beautifully refurbished 2020 Hyundai Elantra.

## What Is the Recycled Rides Program?

The car wasn't built overnight. It started life as a vehicle that had been in an accident — its front end damaged in a collision — and was donated by Allstate to the National Auto Body Council's Recycled Rides for Schools program. Dunwoody's Automotive Collision & Refinishing students spent months repairing dents, repainting panels, and restoring the Elantra SEL to like-new condition.

Since 2007, the NABC Recycled Rides program has repaired and donated over 3,850 vehicles valued at nearly $69 million, working with collision repair shops, insurers, and technical schools across the country. The "For Schools" version gives vocational students hands-on training on real cars while helping people in their community.

## How Far Can One Refurbished Car Go?

For Hanson — a U.S. Navy airman from 1993 to 1997 — the Elantra means he can finally drive to his doctor appointments without relying on others. "Big-time game changer," he said. "I've been walking everywhere to get food. So now I can finally shop at more reasonable places and buy in bulk."

The project brought together the entire automotive industry: Allstate donated the car, Dunwoody students did the repairs, and more than a dozen local businesses — from Caliber Collision to LKQ to Morries Hyundai — contributed parts, services, and expertise. Professor Brian Briske described it as "handing over a tool to help transformation."

## What's the Ripple Effect?

Hanson was nominated for the car by Hennepin County Homeless Access, which helps residents find emergency shelter. The presentation at Dunwoody College let students see firsthand how their work makes a real difference — not just in a classroom, but in someone's daily life.

The program also accepts donated vehicles through its Keys for Change program, where donors receive tax deductions while helping veterans, first responders, and families in need.

## Fun Fact

The word "Elantra" comes from the French word *hélice*, meaning "spiral" or "coil" — originally referring to the spiral motion of a propeller!
