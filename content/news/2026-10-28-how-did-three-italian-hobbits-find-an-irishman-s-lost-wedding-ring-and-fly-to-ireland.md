## How Did Three Italian Hobbits Find an Irishman's Lost Wedding Ring and Fly to Ireland?

Imagine losing something precious — something you've worn every day for 16 years — in the roar of a fast-flowing mountain stream. Then imagine three strangers deciding to make finding it their personal quest, searching for days until they spot that tiny glint of gold in the riverbed. And then, for the grand finale, they hop on a plane dressed as hobbits to hand it back to you in person. That is exactly what happened to Patrick Mohr during a family vacation in Italy.

### 1. The ring takes an unexpected dip

Patrick Mohr was visiting Tuscany with his wife and children, enjoying a sunny afternoon playing in a mountain stream near the town of **Lucca**. As he helped his kids splash around in the cool water, one of the children grabbed his hand a little too enthusiastically — and the sudden tug sent his **18-karat wedding band** sliding off his finger and tumbling downstream into the rushing current.

For 16 years, that simple gold ring had been a daily reminder of his marriage. In an instant, it was gone, carried away by the water.

### 2. Three friends become determined "hobbits"

Mohr's wife quickly looked around for help and met three local young men: **Filippo Palagi, Nicola Malloggi, and Edoardo Poggi**. Although they didn't even have swimming goggles to lend, the friends immediately agreed to try to find the ring. Over the next **three days**, they searched the river from different angles — first by kayak, then by snorkel, roping themselves to boulders in the most treacherous spots.

On the third day, Malloggi spotted a flash of gold among the river sand. He grabbed it and surfaced with the wedding ring sitting right on his palm — as if it had been waiting for them.

### 3. A hobbit's journey to Ireland

Rather than simply mailing the ring back, the three "hobbits" decided the journey to return it should be just as special. They took a **Ryanair flight** to Ireland, arriving at Mohr's doorstep **dressed as hobbits** and singing songs from *The Lord of the Rings* — even greeting him with a photo captioned "Hi Sméagol, look what we found."

Mohr, who was in his kitchen preparing dinner when the text arrived, later said, "sometimes you have to put things out into the universe and ask for help. And sometimes, you get an answer."

As a thank-you, Mohr took his unlikely heroes sightseeing through some of Ireland's most beautiful places: the **Glendalough valley** in the Wicklow Mountains, the **Glenmalure Valley**, and a cozy local pub for a round of Guinness and a traditional Irish dinner.

## Fun Fact

The name "hobbit" was coined by author J.R.R. Tolkien in *The Lord of the Rings* (1937), but the word may have been inspired by an 18th-century English word for "fairy" or "goblin." So when these three Italian friends arrived singing hobbit songs, they were speaking a language of kindness that needed no translation at all.
