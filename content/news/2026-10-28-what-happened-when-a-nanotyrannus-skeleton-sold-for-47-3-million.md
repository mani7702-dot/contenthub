# What happened when a Nanotyrannus skeleton sold for $47.3 million?

## 1. A tiny tyrant's biggest payday

Imagine a dinosaur so fierce it could slice through meat with blade-like teeth — yet small enough to fit in a large room. That is **Nanotyrannus**, and one of the best-preserved skeletons ever found just sold at auction for a staggering **$47.3 million**! The skeleton, nicknamed "Jodi," went under the hammer at Sotheby's in New York, becoming the second-most expensive dinosaur fossil ever sold at auction. It even beat expectations — experts thought it might sell for around $9 million, but six eager bidders drove the final price sky-high in a thrilling 12-minute battle.

## 2. From mistaken identity to scientific star

For decades, scientists were confused about Nanotyrannus. Was it a real dinosaur genus, or just a young Tyrannosaurus rex that hadn't grown up yet? The tiny teeth and slender build led many experts to think it was merely a "teenage T. rex." But in **October 2025**, researchers **Lindsay Zanno and James Napoli** published an exhaustive study that finally put the debate to rest. By carefully studying a near-complete skeleton called "Bloody Mary," they confirmed that Nanotyrannus is its **very own kind of dinosaur** — a mid-sized tyrant about 17 feet (5.2 meters) long, weighing up to 2,600 pounds (1,200 kg) when fully grown. The key clue? Adult Nanotyrannus has **15 to 16 teeth** in each side of its upper jaw, while adult T. rex only has **11 to 12**.

## 3. A fossil found on a Montana ranch

So where did this priceless skeleton come from? It all began in the **Hell Creek Formation** of Montana — a famous rock layer famous for preserving dinosaurs from the very end of the Age of Dinosaurs, about **67 to 66 million years ago**. Rancher **Jodi Pierson** was walking her land when she spotted something extraordinary. Professional fossil hunter **Clayton Phipps** later excavated the nearly complete skeleton, which includes about **191 fossilized bones** and a beautifully preserved skull. The skeleton was first shown to the public in 2023 at an art fair in London, where it was still mistakenly labeled as a juvenile T. rex!

## 4. The big debate: museums vs. private vaults

The record-breaking sale has brought a fierce debate back into the spotlight. Some scientists worry that when fossils sell for tens of millions of dollars, they might disappear into private collections where **students and young explorers can no longer see them**. As paleontologist **Steve Brusatte** told the BBC: "Imagine if the Mona Lisa was owned by one wealthy person and locked away — that is what could happen to Jodi." On the other side of the argument, others point out that commercial fossil hunters invest huge amounts of time and money into finding and preserving these delicate remains. They hope the skeleton will eventually find its way into a public museum where it can inspire future generations.

## Fun Fact

Nanotyrannus was NOT the first dinosaur to sell for a jaw-dropping price. In July 2026, another T. rex skeleton nicknamed "Gus" sold for an even higher **$50.1 million** — currently the most expensive fossil ever sold. And in July 2024, a Stegosaurus named "Apex" fetched a dazzling **$44.6 million**, proving that dinosaur bones really are "roar-some" treasures!
