Sea turtles are the great wanderers of the ocean. Some swim thousands of kilometres, using Earth's magnetic field, the sun and shifting currents as their map, often returning to the very beach where they hatched to lay their own eggs. That reputation made one group of turtles in the Galápagos especially surprising.

## A surprising homebody

A team of scientists set out to learn where a particular kind of green sea turtle spends its time. These turtles, called "yellow-morph" green turtles, are one of two genetically distinct forms of the species. The team captured nine adults — seven males and two females — in shallow water near four Galápagos islands in 2015, gave them nicknames, and fitted them with small electronic tags that sent their positions to satellites.

Then they waited and watched. For up to 418 days each, the tagged turtles were followed across the 198,000-square-kilometre Galápagos Marine Reserve.

## They barely left the neighbourhood

The turtles mostly stayed in shallow coastal water. A few occasionally wandered as far as 25 kilometres from shore, but most remained much closer to the islands. Five of the nine stayed mainly around a single island. Two turtles, Bernardo and Sara, moved between Isabela and San Cristóbal, while Leandro travelled between Isabela and Española. The most adventurous, Ainoa, was tagged near San Cristóbal before heading to Santa Cruz and then Isabela.

Interestingly, every recorded trip between islands happened during the cooler months, from April through October. On average, each turtle used a home range of about 1,498 square kilometres — though that number varied a lot from one animal to another.

## Why the surprise matters

Scientists had expected these turtles to migrate to distant beaches in the western Pacific to breed, yet not one of the tagged animals left the marine reserve during the study. That does not mean they never travel. Female yellow-morph turtles are not known to nest on Galápagos beaches, so the researchers believe they eventually leave to return to their birth beaches far away.

The discovery matters for conservation. Because individual turtles may depend on waters around several different islands — and on the open corridors between them — protecting just one favourite feeding spot may not be enough. The researchers say habitat should stay healthy across all the islands, not just one.

## Fun fact

Green sea turtles are the only one of the world's seven living sea turtle species that is mostly vegetarian as an adult. They munch on seagrasses and algae — and the younger yellow-morph turtles in this study grew faster than their black-morph cousins, a difference scientists still cannot fully explain.
