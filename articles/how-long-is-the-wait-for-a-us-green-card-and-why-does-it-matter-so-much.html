How long is the wait for a US green card — and why does it matter so much?

2026-10-224

Millions of people around the world hope to live and work in the United States with a green card. For highly skilled Indian tech workers, the wait can stretch for decades — what makes the line so long?

How long is the wait for a US green card — and why does it matter so much?

A dream job that needs a special key

Around the world, many people study hard in school hoping to work in their dream job. In countries like India, a very popular dream is to earn an engineering degree, then get a job at a big tech company in the United States. The prize at the end of that path is something called a green card — a special plastic card that lets you live and work in the US forever, almost like being a citizen.

But here is the problem: there are only so many green cards available each year, and millions of people want one. That means most folks have to get into a very long line.

Why the line moves so slowly

The United States gives out green cards through a lottery-like system. Each year, a fixed number of employment-based green cards are handed out, and they are split fairly evenly between all the countries of the world. Because India and China send huge numbers of skilled workers, the line for people from those countries becomes extremely long.

According to a 2026 report from the National Foundation for American Policy, about one million Indians are waiting for employment-based green cards right now — and they make up 79% of everyone waiting worldwide. Depending on which part of the line you are in, the report says the wait can be as short as five years… or as staggeringly long as 179 years.

That is not a typo — 179 years. Longer than a hundred generations of students have been alive!

New rules, new worries

Recently, the US government announced a fresh rule: eight large technology companies — including Microsoft from the US and India's Infosys, Tata Consultancy Services, Wipro and HCL Technologies — were told they could no longer use a common shortcut to sponsor workers for green cards. That shortcut (called PERM) lets a company prove it could not find an American worker first, then fast-track a foreign worker's application. Freezing this shortcut means the line moves even more slowly for everyone.

Numbers show how big the change is: Tata Consultancy Services filed 1,804 green-card applications in 2023, dropped to 513 in 2024, and filed only 2 in 2025-26. That is a fall of almost 99%.

So what does this look like for families?

It is not just about paperwork. Many families moved to the US years ago, their children grew up there going to American schools, speaking English with their friends, and celebrating American holidays. Now, parents who have been waiting for a green card for many years face a painful choice: stay in limbo, or move back to a country their children may barely remember.

Immigration experts say that if the rules keep tightening, some people may indeed return home — and that some work may move with them, since companies can simply do the same jobs from elsewhere.

Try this at home

Build the line yourself. Take 20 toy figures and tell the class each one wants a single cookie (your "green card"). You only have 3 cookies to give out this week. Hand them out — but then make two rules: (1) no more than one cookie per classroom, and (2) people from some classrooms have to wait far longer even if they were first in line. Watch how unhappy the line grows. That's a simple model of how the green-card system treats fairness and scarcity.

Fun Fact

The phrase "green card" has nothing to do with the colour green! It comes from the green tinted photograph printed on US permanent-resident cards long ago. Today the card is plastic and the photo is in colour — but everyone still calls it a "green card" anyway.
