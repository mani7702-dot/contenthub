How Did World Countries Agree to Protect 30% of Earth by 2030?

2026-10-31T07:42:00+05:304

At the COP17 biodiversity summit in Yerevan, Armenia, countries reached a deal to implement the 2022 Global Biodiversity Framework — but the big missing piece is money.

1. Where Did the Talks Happen?

In the autumn of 2026, nearly 200 countries gathered in Yerevan, Armenia, for COP17 — the 37th meeting of the Convention on Biological Diversity. The goal was to put the 2022 Global Biodiversity Framework's "30x30" target into practice: protecting 30% of Earth's land and oceans by the end of the decade.

2. What Was Agreed?

After days of negotiation, countries settled on the key parts of a new action plan. The deal acknowledges that the world is currently on track to meet only one of the framework's 24 targets. The main barrier? Money.

Countries have already put 623 million acres of degraded land into protection — an area larger than Greenland. Recent success stories include the recovery of elephants, green sea turtles, and tigers, praised by the WWF. The comeback of bluefin tuna in the Mediterranean has also shown that nature can bounce back when given the chance.

3. The Money Problem

Back in 2022, nations agreed to put $30 billion toward nature recovery by 2030, plus another $170 billion from private sources. But years later, many countries have been redirecting aid budgets to defence spending and energy costs, leaving pledges behind schedule.

At this year's meeting, more than $1 billion was pledged — with half coming from philanthropists including Leonardo DiCaprio's Re:wild organization. However, the "Cali Fund," which asks companies that profit from using genetic material from nature to contribute, had only raised $10,000 at the start of the talks.

4. What Comes Next?

A separate but related challenge emerged: some countries, including Saudi Arabia and Russia, frustrated efforts to approve scientific reports on the state of nature. French Ecological Transition Minister Monique Barluquet called these "attacks on science."

Next month, countries will meet again for COP31 — the annual climate conference — where the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change faces its own funding and approval challenges.

Fun Fact

The WWF noted that three species once on the brink — African elephants, green sea turtles, and tigers — have all shown significant population recoveries in recent years thanks to international protection efforts. The bluefin tuna, once nearly wiped out by overfishing, has returned to abundance in the Mediterranean after strict quotas were introduced.
