How Did a Simple Roll of Tape Beat Sandbags at Stopping Flood Water?

2026-10-29T18:30:00+05:303 min

Tests at the University of Hull found that everyday synthetic tape can block up to 99% of flood water from sneaking through a doorway — far better than heavy, clumsy sandbags.

How Did a Simple Roll of Tape Beat Sandbags at Stopping Flood Water?

1. What Happened in the Test Lab?

When flood warnings flash across your phone, every minute counts. You grab whatever you can find — often those heavy, dusty sandbags that feel like lifting a brick. But what if a lightweight roll of tape hiding in your toolbox could do a better job?

Researchers at the University of Hull's state-of-the-art Property Flood Resilience lab put common household and hardware-store tapes to the test. They built a realistic doorway and slowly poured water at it, measuring how much leaked through.

2. The Surprising Winners

Out of every tape they tried, synthetic rubber tape was the champion — it let through just 1% of the water, meaning it blocked 99%. That is like catching 99 out of every 100 raindrops that try to sneak past your door.

Duct tape came in a close second, holding back 97–98% of the water. Even the worst-performing tape still managed to keep out 80%, while traditional sandbags only managed 70% under the same conditions.

3. Why Tape Works Better

Sandbags are heavy, awkward, and — as the researchers point out — they often start to rot or fall apart after sitting in storage for months. Tape, on the other hand, is light, cheap, and easy to store in a drawer until you need it.

The trick is applying the tape to the outside of the door rather than the inside. When pressed firmly around the frame, it seals gaps that water loves to creep through. The university also found that outward-opening doors naturally resisted leakage better than inward-opening ones — a 74% improvement even before any tape was added.

4. Who Asked for This Research?

The study was commissioned by Flood Re, a joint initiative between the UK government and home insurers. Their goal: give families simple, affordable advice they can act on in a hurry.

The Environment Agency estimates that 4.6 million people in the UK face the risk of surface-water flooding, which is the fastest-growing flood threat for households. Having an easy, inexpensive defense ready can make all the difference while emergency crews arrive.
