A simple touch can bring dying corals back to life in 7 weeks

2026-10-203 min

Scientists found that a bleached, whitening coral can fully recover if it simply touches a healthy coral from the same family. It is the fastest comeback from bleaching ever recorded.

Some of the saddest sights in the ocean right now are white, ghostly corals. But a new experiment shows something hopeful: a bleached coral can come all the way back to life just by touching a healthy coral from the same family.

In the lab, corals that had turned white and were close to dying became fully healthy again within seven weeks. Scientists say it is the fastest recovery from bleaching ever recorded.

What bleaching is

Most corals look colorful, but their color does not come from the coral itself. Tiny algae live inside corals and give them food and color. When the water gets too warm, corals push those algae out and turn white. That is bleaching, and without the algae, a coral slowly starves.

The surprise in the tank

Researcher Danny Collins was studying coral fragments in a tank when two of them tipped over and landed touching each other. One was bleached; the other was healthy, and they came from the same coral animal, so they were genetic twins. Where they touched, color came back to the bleached coral.

So Collins set up a careful test. He made pairs of fragments — one healthy, one bleached — and let half of the pairs touch, while keeping the other half a few millimeters apart.

Here is the difference: every bleached coral that touched its healthy twin recovered completely within seven weeks. Every one kept apart died.

How a hug heals

When genetic twins touch, their tissues actually fuse together. Through that bridge, the healthy coral shares its helpful algae with the sick one. Color started returning within days.

Some coral pairs were separated after just a week of contact — and even they made a full recovery. That tiny infection of algae was enough to save them.

One big catch

There is an honest limit here. It only works with corals that are the same genetic clone, touching in real contact. That is hard to do for a whole wild reef. So this is not a fix for ocean warming — the real fix is slowing warming itself.

But it is a new clue: corals are tougher than we thought, and a little kindness between coral cousins can pull a family back from the edge.
