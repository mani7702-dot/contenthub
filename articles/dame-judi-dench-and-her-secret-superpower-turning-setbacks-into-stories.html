Dame Judi Dench and her secret superpower: turning setbacks into stories

2026-10-224 min

Oscar-winning actress Dame Judi Dench, 91, says her eye condition stopped her from reading and performing — but her seven-decade career is full of stories that show how curiosity and humor win every time.

Dame Judi Dench and her secret superpower: turning setbacks into stories

If you have ever watched James Bond movies, you have met Dame Judi Dench. On screen she plays a no-nonsense boss who cuts through nonsense — and off screen, she is just as sharp.

A career built on curiosity

Dame Judi (that is how people address her) has been acting for over 70 years. She started in 1957 with a theatre group in England and never looked back.

She played Lady Macbeth in Macbeth, Juliet in Romeo and Juliet, and then — the one everyone remembers — “M”, Bond’s boss in GoldenEye (1995). She became the first woman to play the role, and she stayed in it for eight Bond movies until 2015.

In 1999, she won an Academy Award (that is an Oscar) for playing Queen Elizabeth I in Shakespeare in Love. She has also won six BAFTA Awards, two Golden Globes, and seven Olivier Awards — more trophies than most people can name on one hand.

But here is the funny thing: when she talks about all this, she does it like she is telling you about her Tuesday.

The eyes that see differently

More than ten years ago, doctors told Dench she had age-related macular degeneration — a condition that makes the center of your vision blurry. It is not a disease that makes you sick; it just changes how clearly you can see.

“I can’t be on stage,” she told BBC Radio 4. “I can’t do a play anymore. I can’t do a film anymore. I can’t read anymore.”

When she walks into a room, she sometimes sees the wrong person and almost gives them a hug. “It is rather awkward,” she laughed. “I just apologize.”

She turned 91 last December. Ageing, she says, is “nothing special.” But her mind is as quick as ever — she just reads scripts out loud with her grandson now instead of by herself.

Stories that last longer than fame

Dench just released a new memoir (that is a book of memories) called Is it too late to make a run for it? — a title she borrowed from a line she heard in a play long ago.

In it, she tells stories like the time a former spy invited her to lunch and the driver could not find her house, or when she met Sidney Poitier backstage and stood speechless in a “wind tunnel” of starlight.

She still loves Shakespeare. She says his words move her “like the rhythm of your heart,” and every October she remembers watching her brother play King Duncan in Macbeth when she was nine — the moment she decided she wanted to act.

Why she matters

In a world that often forgets the names of yesterday’s stars, Dame Judi Dench is still being asked for interviews. She is still signing books. She is still making people laugh — even when she reads the wrong tele-teleprompter.

And maybe that is the real superpower: not that she remembers every role, but that she still finds a story worth telling in every day.

Try this at home

Ask a grown-up to read you a favorite story out loud — maybe a page from James Bond or Shakespeare. Notice how the voice changes when someone reads aloud. Then ask: what is a story you have heard that you will never forget? Write it down or draw it. Stories are gifts — and the best ones are the ones you pass on.

Fun fact

Dame Judi Dench is related to Tycho Brahe, the famous astronomer from 400 years ago — through her Danish great-great-great-grandfather. That means every time you look at the stars and wonder what is out there, you are thinking along the same stardust trail as Dench’s family tree!
