How an AI 'litter dashcam' is mapping Britain's roadside trash

2026-10-273 min

A software developer built an AI-powered 'litter dashcam' that automatically spots and grades roadside trash. It has already logged over 28,000 pieces of litter across the UK — and the data is helping environmental groups demand cleaner streets.

How an AI 'litter dashcam' is mapping Britain's roadside trash

You know how sometimes you're in the car and your parent says, "Don't throw anything out the window"? Well, imagine if your dashcam could see all the trash by the roadside and count it for you — automatically!

1. A frustrated developer builds a solution

Robin Bennett is a software developer from Shropshury, England. He started noticing something that made him sad: there was a lot of rubbish alongside the roads. So many plastic bottles, food wrappers, and bags just laying around. He thought, there must be a better way to track this and show how big the problem really is.

2. Meet the "litter dashcam"

Bennett built something he calls the Roadside Litter app. Here's how it works:

You mount your phone on the dashboard, press start, and the screen goes black so it doesn't distract the driver. Then, every 10 seconds, the camera takes a photo. The app's AI looks at each photo and asks: "What kind of litter is that?" It can spot bottles, bags, wrappers — and it grades the amount of trash using a national standard.

After each ride, the app uploads the data. Already, it has found over 3,000 litter reports in Shropshire alone — and more than 28,000 across the whole United Kingdom!

3. The hotspots

Bennett discovered that litter hangs out in the same places over and over. Fast-food wrappers pile up near convenience stores and takeaway shops. Empty bottles gather near motorway service stations. And guess what? The AI dashcam confirmed it with hard numbers.

His data showed that the problem wasn't just in his hometown — it was a wider national issue. That's exactly what he wanted to find out.

4. Sharing the data to make change

Bennett shared his AI dashcam findings with an environmental group called Clean Up Britain. By showing exactly where and how much litter exists, the data helps councils and environmental groups know where to focus their cleanup efforts.

Shropshire Council is even exploring new ways to improve litter management on busy roads — all because one developer got curious about the trash he kept seeing.

Try this at home

Go on a neighborhood walk (with an adult!) and spend 10 minutes counting how many pieces of litter you can spot. Is it near a store? A bus stop? A park? Try the same walk a week later after a community cleanup. You'll be doing citizen science — the same thing scientists do when they study the world around us!

Fun fact

The most littered item in the entire world? Cigarette butts. About 4.5 trillion of them get thrown away each year — that's more than the number of people on Earth!
