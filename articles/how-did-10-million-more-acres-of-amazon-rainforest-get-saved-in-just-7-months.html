How Did 10 Million More Acres of Amazon Rainforest Get Saved in Just 7 Months?

2026-10-274 min

An American nonprofit teamed up with a Bolivian conservation group to create a 715,000-acre wildlife reserve along the Iténez River, a crucial corridor that connects protected areas across Brazil and Bolivia. The new reserve pushes the partner organization's total protected land to 70 million acres — an area the size of Nevada — with 10 million acres gained in just seven months.

How Did 10 Million More Acres of Amazon Rainforest Get Saved in Just 7 Months?

A river that stitches two countries together

Imagine a giant green seam running along the edge of two countries — one thread in a vast, living tapestry. That is the Iténez River, a 780-mile waterway that flows right along the border between Bolivia and Brazil, eventually feeding into the Amazon River itself. Along its banks and in the forests around it, an American nonprofit called the Rainforest Trust has just helped create a new protected haven: the Agua Dulce Municipal Wildlife Reserve, covering 715,000 acres of rainforest, wetlands, and grasslands.

What lives in this river corridor?

The Iténez region is a wildlife crossroads. Scientists have recorded 260 species of fish in its waters alone, including 25 types found nowhere else on Earth. On land, the reserve is home to some of the Amazon's most iconic residents: jaguars that slip silently through the undergrowth, gentle Amazon river dolphins that glow pink in the shallows, and mighty giant otters that family groups protect as fiercely as any guardian. Lowland tapirs and black caimans round out a cast of creatures that depend on this corridor to move between feeding grounds, nesting sites, and safe havens.

The reserve connects a chain of existing protected areas — from Bolivia's Noel Kempff Mercado National Park to Brazil's Corumbiara State Park and Ilha das Flores State Park. Together, these parks and reserves form a continuous path that animals can follow as seasons change and food becomes scarce.

Why this matters for the planet

The Rainforest Trust says its work has now directly protected 70 million acres of rainforest — an area roughly the size of Nevada. What is even more remarkable is that 10 million of those acres were added in just seven months. That pace shows what is possible when urgency meets action.

"The world has pledged to protect 30% of the planet by 2030," said James Deutsch, CEO of the Rainforest Trust. "As that deadline approaches, every acre counts — especially the most imperiled ones where conservation can make the greatest difference for both species and people."

At the same time, Brazilian authorities reported this week that deforestation in the Amazon has dropped to its lowest rate ever, suggesting that expanded protections are starting to pay off. Rainforests act like the planet's lungs, pulling carbon dioxide from the air and storing it in their trees. Every acre preserved is acre of carbon kept out of the atmosphere.

Try this at home

Draw a wildlife corridor. On a large sheet of paper, draw two national parks separated by a road or a field. Now sketch a winding "wildlife bridge" — maybe a tunnel, a green overpass, or a river path — that lets animals move safely between them. Label the animals that might use it: deer, foxes, butterflies, or birds. You have just designed your own corridor, just like the Iténez River does for jaguars and otters!

Fun Fact

The Iténez River is part of the Madeira River basin, one of the Amazon's largest tributary systems. Its waters flow for hundreds of miles before reaching the mighty Amazon — which means the health of the Agua Dulce Reserve affects not just the animals that live there, but the entire downstream ecosystem, all the way to the Amazon River's mouth!

for-grownups: The Agua Dulce Municipal Wildlife Reserve in Bolivia was established with support from the Rainforest Trust and the Bolivian NGO Faunagua. Covering 715,000 acres (292,000 hectares) along the Iténez River, the reserve protects a transboundary ecological corridor connecting Noel Kempff Mercado National Park (Bolivia) with Corumbiara State Park and Ilha das Flores State Park (Brazil). The Iténez River (also known as the Guaporé) is 1,260 km (780 miles) long, with 570 miles forming the Brazil-Bolivia border, and is part of the Madeira River basin that feeds the Amazon. The reserve encompasses Amazonian forests, floodplains, pampas, and riparian ecosystems under three pillars of landscape conservation: wildlife protection, ecosystem service maintenance, and sustainable local extraction. Rainforest Trust, a US-based nonprofit founded in 1988, has protected over 50 million acres globally through partnerships in Latin America, with $46 million in revenue in 2023. Recent Brazilian data shows Amazon deforestation has fallen to its lowest recorded rate, aligning with the 30x30 global biodiversity target.
