How to pick a book you will actually finish

ReadingContentHub2026-10-163 min

Picking a book is harder than it looks. A cover can lie. A back page can lie. Even the first chapter can lie. Here are three rules that work.

Rule 1: Read the first two pages out loud

If your tongue trips over the words, the rest of the book will trip too. The first two pages tell you how the whole book will sound. If it sounds like a friend you would listen to, keep going. If it sounds like homework, close it.

Rule 2: Give it fifty pages, no more

Fifty pages is a fair trial. Some books start slow on purpose and then take off — but the ones that are truly wrong for you feel wrong from early on. Fifty pages is enough time to tell the difference between "slow" and "not for me".

Rule 3: Stopping is allowed

A book you did not finish is not a failure. It is information: you now know more about what you like. Readers who stop books read more books. Put it back on the shelf with a clear conscience and pick the next one.

Try this next time you are at the library: read the first two pages of three books you have never opened. One of them will sound like a friend.
