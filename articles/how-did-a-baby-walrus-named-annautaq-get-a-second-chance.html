How Did a Baby Walrus Named Annautaq Get a Second Chance?

2026-10-273 min

time: 14:32

A baby walrus found alone on a remote Alaska beach has a new home — and a name to remember: Annautaq.

The Big Find

Wildlife researcher Violet Anniskett and U.S. Geological Survey scientist Tony Fischbach were walking a northern Alaska beach when they spotted something unexpected: a tiny, 220-pound walrus calf, all alone. The little calf had been left behind after a walrus herd moved on during heavy surf. With scavenging birds circling, the calf needed help fast. The two rescuers knew the 5-month-old would not survive without care — so they sprang into action.

A Long Trip South

Because walrus calves cannot survive on their own without mothers, wildlife experts determined the best place for Annautaq was SeaWorld Orlando — one of only four U.S. facilities that can house walruses. Getting there was no easy trip: the calf needed to travel 4,000 miles from Alaska to Florida. Along the way, he was given around-the-clock care, including up to six bottle feedings per day, all while passing a planned 30-day quarantine to keep the resident animals healthy.

What This Means for Walruses

The rescue shows why scientists say protecting walrus habitats matters. Pacific walruses (_Odobenus rosmarus divergens_) are the only species in the family Odobenidae, and they depend completely on their mothers for milk and protection during their first year of life. By helping orphaned calves like Annautaq, researchers and caretakers learn more about how to protect the whole species.

Now at SeaWorld, Annautaq will meet Waldo, Kaboodle, and Uki — other rescued walruses who help him grow and learn. As he gets stronger, he will also become an ambassador, helping visitors understand how special walruses are and what we can do to keep them safe.

Try this at home

You can help marine animals by visiting aquariums and zoos that focus on rescue and education. You can also reduce plastic use — less plastic in the ocean means healthier waters for every sea creature.

Fun Fact

A baby walrus is called a calf — just like a baby whale or dolphin! And the name "Annautaq" means "axe" in the Iñupiaq language, honoring the rescuer's father's heritage.
