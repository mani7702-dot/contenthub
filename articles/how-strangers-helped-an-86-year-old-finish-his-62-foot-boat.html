How Strangers Helped an 86-Year-Old Finish His 62-Foot Boat

2026-10-223

>-

The Yard Boat That Dreamed of the Sea

Imagine starting a project when Friends was just beginning, and still working on it nearly 40 years later. That's exactly what Dale, an 86-year-old from Mineral Wells, Texas, did when he began welding a 62-foot steel boat right in his front yard back in 1987.

His goal was simple and grand at the same time: finish the boat and sail it to Alaska.

Forty Years of Welding

Dale didn't just tack on pieces — he built the whole thing himself, sheet by sheet, dreaming of distant waters. The vessel now has four staterooms and is roughly 75% complete. But as Dale grew older, the enormous task felt increasingly out of reach.

The finished boat sits on a steep hill between yard and road, and moving it would need heavy machinery — something one person can't easily manage alone.

A Ladder Climb Changed Everything

Everything shifted when three English visitors — Laurence Benson, Benjie, and Will — happened upon the yard. They were told about the boat and stopped by unannounced.

When Dale started talking about the project, his passion was unmistakable.

"The second he started talking about the boat, you could see it," Benson wrote. "He's as passionate now as he was 40 years ago. His dream is to see her on the water."

Dale even climbed a ladder to show them the deck — something he hadn't done in years. Moved by the decades of dedication, Benson (a content creator) and his friends launched a GoFundMe to cover finishing work, equipment to get the boat down the hill, and transport to a launch site.

The Kindness Engine

Within days, the campaign raised more than $41,000 toward its $45,000 goal from over 1,500 donors. About $50,000 is needed to get the boat moved and on the water.

It's a reminder of how far a little help can go when someone has poured decades into something they love. Dale's boat may not have left the yard yet — but thanks to strangers who believed in a 40-year-old dream, it's closer than ever to finally seeing open water.

Try this at home

Build your own mini dream project! Grab cardboard, tape, and a ruler. Design a tiny boat — it can be as fancy or as simple as you like. Name it and give it a destination: "The S.S. Homework Helper" sailing to the Land of Finished Assignments, or "The Cardboard Cruiser" heading to the Island of Weekend Fun. Measure and cut, then test it in a sink or bathtub (with an adult watching). How does the shape of your boat's hull stay afloat? Try making it wider, or adding a small sail from a piece of paper.

Fun fact

Dale isn't the only person building a giant boat in their yard! There's a whole subculture of "backyard boat builders" worldwide. Some build full-size replicas of famous ships. One British man built a 36-foot replica of the Titanic in his garden shed (complete with the ship's name on the bow) — and sold it on eBay for £45,000! Dale's 62-footer is nearly twice that size.
