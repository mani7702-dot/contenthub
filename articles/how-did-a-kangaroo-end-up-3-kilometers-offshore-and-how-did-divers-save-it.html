How Did a Kangaroo End Up 3 Kilometers Offshore — And How Did Divers Save It?

2026-10-294 min

A routine fishing trip in Boston Bay, Australia, turned into an extraordinary wildlife rescue when a dive crew spotted an exhausted kangaroo swimming over 3 kilometers from shore. Here's how they helped the waterlogged marsupial back to dry land.

How Did a Kangaroo End Up 3 Kilometers Offshore — And How Did Divers Save It?

Imagine you are out on a calm sea, checking fish farms in your boat, when the skipper suddenly shouts, “I reckon there's a roo in the water!” That is exactly what happened to a group of divers off the coast of South Australia — and their quick thinking gave an exhausted kangaroo a second chance at life.

1. A Surprising Spot in the Distance

Jake Murray, a scuba diver and farmer, and his crew were working aboard their dive boat in Boston Bay, near Port Lincoln, South Australia. They were far from the shoreline — about three kilometers (roughly 1.9 miles) offshore — when their skipper, Ryan Smith, noticed something unusual floating on the gentle waves.

At first, the crew thought it might be a piece of debris or a piece of seaweed. But as they drew closer, the “debris” blinked back at them. It was a kangaroo — a large, waterlogged marsupial that was clearly struggling to stay afloat. Kangaroos are excellent swimmers, but this one had been treading water for far too long.

2. A Gentle Rescue

The boat had a special feature — a “sea door” that lowers down to the waterline. This made it possible for the crew to ease the kangaroo aboard without stressing the animal further. Murray described how he carefully cradled the exhausted marsupial, making sure to control its powerful legs (which kangaroos use like built-in springs on land).

“It was pretty knackered, pretty waterlogged,” Murray said. “I was just kind of cradling him there for a while, and it was almost like he was having a little nap.”

Among the rescue team was Miguel Villarroel, a diver visiting from the Galápagos Islands, who later joked that this was the first time he had ever seen a kangaroo — even though he had been in Australia for some time.

3. Back on Dry Land

Once the boat turned back toward the shore, Murray and his crew kept the kangaroo calm and comfortable. After they reached shallow water near the beach, the kangaroo was gently guided onto the sand. After resting for a few minutes and shaking off the water, the resilient marsupial bounced (or “hopped”) away under its own strength — free to return to whatever adventure had led it so far out to sea in the first place.

Fun Fact

Kangaroos can swim using a doggy-paddle motion, and they often use water as a refuge from heat or predators. But venturing three kilometers offshore is unusual even for a tough Australian kangaroo — making this rescue one for the books!

Fun fact: The word “kangaroo” comes from the Guugu Yimithirr language of northern Queensland, where the word gangur referred to a specific type of large marsupial. When a British shipwreck survivor asked local Aboriginal people what the hopping animals were called in 1770, they supposedly replied “I don’t know” — but it actually sounded like “kangaroo” to the European ear!
