How Did a New Yorker Named Service Clean Up a Brooklyn Dumping Site All on His Own?

2026-10-29T04:59:00+05:303

When Joshua Service spotted a filthy dumping site under a Brooklyn bridge, he didn't just call 311 — he grabbed gloves and cleaned it up himself, filling ten trash bags in two hours and inspiring his whole neighborhood.

How Did a New Yorker Named Service Clean Up a Brooklyn Dumping Site All on His Own?

It Started With a Commute

Every day, Joshua Service rides the Long Island Rail Road from Queens to his job in Crown Heights. Last week, as he walked under the Nostrand Avenue station near Atlantic Avenue in Brooklyn, he noticed something that made him stop in his tracks: a mountain of trash — old shoes, broken furniture, discarded bags — piled high under the bridge.

Instead of walking past, he pulled out his phone and posted a video calling out the city's 311 number, wondering why no one had cleaned it up. The response from strangers online was quick and clear: "Why don't you do it yourself?"

Ten Bags of Garbage in Two Hours

So he did.

For two hours on a Monday morning, Service put on a pair of gloves, grabbed a rake, and started filling trash bags. After just 120 minutes of work, he had hauled away about ten full bags of garbage. Among the finds were women's shoes, children's shoes, and piles of household waste — evidence, he believes, of someone regularly dumping trash there from a vehicle.

When passersby asked if he worked for the Department of Transportation, he laughed and said, "No, man, I'm an independent contractor."

More Than Just Trash

But Service's cleanup did more than tidy a street corner. When the city's Department of Homeless Services later visited the area, they found a homeless resident there and were able to connect that person with shelter. The underpass that was once a dumping ground is now clean — and someone who needed help is off the streets.

Local resident Haile Ali stopped to thank Service in person. "Phenomenal," he said. "It was philanthropic. He was passionate about it and cleaned it up. It's something we need more of."

City Councilmember Chi Ossé even announced plans to honor Service at the very spot where he did the work.

Why One Person Can Make a Difference

Service says the experience taught him a simple lesson: no matter who you are, you can make a change. "We as a people make our community," he reflects. And who knows? With a last name like Service, maybe a future in public service is written in the stars.

Fun Fact

Joshua Service's last name isn't a coincidence — it comes from a long line of public servants. His great-great-grandfather was one of the first African American conductors on the Pennsylvania Railroad, and his grandmother worked as a city school crossing guard for over 30 years.
