Why did Meta have to pay a record $25 billion — and what it means for your online privacy

2026-10-224 min

A U.S. judge ordered Meta to pay $25 billion for misleading people about how their data is used — the largest penalty of its kind.

1. A very expensive lesson about online rules

Imagine if you told your teacher, "My backpack is completely empty — nothing is hidden inside!" but you were secretly storing snacks, toys, and homework from friends in there. What would happen? Probably a chat with the principal!

That is basically what happened to Meta — the company that runs Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp. A judge in New Mexico said Meta told users and the public that their personal information was safe and never shared with outsiders. But the judge said that was not true. So Meta had to pay a really big fine.

2. The big number: $25,000,000,000

The number is almost impossible to imagine. $25 billion is more money than most countries spend on their schools in a whole year. Judge Francis Mathew said Meta's actions were "willful" — that means on purpose — and they went on for more than eleven years.

Before you worry: this fine is not about you. It is about a company that made promises it did not keep. The judge wanted to send a message: no matter how big you are, there are rules everyone must follow.

3. How did this start? The Cambridge Analytica story

This all began with something called the Cambridge Analytica scandal. A company named Cambridge Analytica found a way to collect information from millions of Facebook users — things like their likes, friendships, and interests — without those users fully knowing.

Why does this matter? Because that information was then used to try to influence elections. It is like if someone peeked at everyone's notes before a test and then told the teacher which answers would be on the quiz.

4. What the judge said

Lawyers for New Mexico said Meta executives, including Mark Zuckerberg, made public statements saying user data was "never shared" — but it actually was shared. The jury agreed. The judge said the fine should "hurt a little" so the company would do better in the future.

Meta said it plans to appeal the decision, calling it "all but impossible to do business" in New Mexico. Even with the appeal, the case is a reminder that when companies make promises about your privacy, those promises matter.

Try this at home

Digital diary test: At home, try keeping a simple diary for one day. Every time you use an app or website, write down what you think it might be learning about you (what you like, what you search for, what you watch). Then, ask a grown-up to help you read the "Privacy Policy" or "Terms of Service" — you will be amazed how much information companies collect, and how few kids realize it!

Fun Fact

The $25 billion fine is bigger than the GDP of 23 countries in the world, including the Cayman Islands and Iceland!

For-grownups: This fine stems from a 2026 New Mexico lawsuit tracing back to the 2018 Cambridge Analytica data scandal. Wikipedia's "Facebook–Cambridge Analytica data scandal" article confirms the 2019 FTC settlement ($5 billion) and the 2022 $725 million class-action settlement. BBC News reporter Kali Hays confirms Judge Francis Mathew reduced the calculated $219 billion penalty to $25 billion.
