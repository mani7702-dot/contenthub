How Did a Luxury Cruise Ship End Up Stranded in Cambodia?

Imagine living on a floating home that was sailing around the entire planet — and then one day, you're asked to pack your bags and stay in a hotel in a country you'd never planned to visit. That's exactly what happened to dozens of passengers aboard the Villa Vie Odyssey, a luxury residential cruise ship that has been cruising the globe since October 2024.

A Grand Dream Put on Pause

The Villa Vie Odyssey set sail from Belfast, Northern Ireland, with an ambitious plan: a continuous 3.5-year voyage covering nearly 150 countries. The ship — nearly 200 meters long and capable of carrying up to 929 guests — was designed for long-term living, complete with bars, restaurants, a pool deck, and even a pickleball court. Residents could rent a cabin for about $3,500 a month or purchase a permanent home for between $120,000 and $440,000.

But as the ship sailed through Asia, fuel prices began climbing steeply. The operator, Villa Vie Residences, explained that the monthly fuel cost per cabin jumped from about $1,800 to as much as $8,000 in parts of Asia. Even after slowing down and spending more time in port to save fuel, the costs proved too much to handle.

Passengers Adapt to Unexpected Circumstances

When the ship docked in the Cambodian port city of Sihanoukville, passengers were told they would need to disembark for the next 60 days while the company worked on restructuring and recapitalizing. Roughly 200 passengers found themselves suddenly in a place none of them had on their original itinerary.

One passenger, Holly Hennessey, told the BBC from Cambodia: "It was not on my bingo card of places where I'd be spending a lot of time." She and her cat Captain were among those settling into temporary hotel accommodations, waiting to hear whether their floating home would set sail again.

What Comes Next?

The company has promised to spend the next two months working on a plan to keep the voyage going, though they cautioned that no outcome is guaranteed. For passengers who've already invested years of their lives (and considerable sums of money) into this floating adventure, the coming weeks will be a test of patience — and optimism.

Fun fact: The Villa Vie Odyssey wasn't always called that. Originally named the Crown Dynasty, the ship was built in 1993 and is powered by four Wärtsilä diesel engines, according to maritime records. It's been transformed since then from a conventional cruise ship into a residential vessel designed for long-term living at sea.
