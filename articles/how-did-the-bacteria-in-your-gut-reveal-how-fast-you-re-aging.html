How Did the Bacteria in Your Gut Reveal How Fast You're Aging?

2026-10-304 min

Scientists discovered that the trillions of microbes living in your digestive system can predict your body's biological aging speed — and one friendly bacterium called Bifidobacterium adolescentis stands out as the strongest helper of them all.

How Did the Bacteria in Your Gut Reveal How Fast You're Aging?

1. Your Gut Bugs as a Biological Clock

Inside your digestive system live trillions of tiny microbes — bacteria, fungi, and other microorganisms that scientists call the "gut microbiome." These friendly helpers don't just help you digest your food; it turns out they also carry hints about how quickly your body is aging on the inside.

A new study from the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa found that the mix of microbes in your gut can predict something called "biological aging" — how fast your body's systems are wearing down — without even needing to know your chronological age. That means gut bacteria might one day help tell whether someone's body is aging faster or slower than their birthday would suggest.

2. The Hunt for Age-Defying Microbes

To find out which microbes matter most, researchers collected both stool and blood samples from 123 volunteers aged 17 to 82. They used DNA methylation data — chemical tags on genes that change as we age — to measure each person's biological age through a tool called DunedinPACE.

Then they ran powerful computer models to see which gut microbes showed up most importantly in the predictions. Two species stood out above all others.

Bifidobacterium adolescentis was the top helper — higher levels of this bacterium were linked to slower biological aging. It was the single most important microbe in the model, contributing about 2.5 times more prediction power than the next runner-up.

On the other side, Succinivibrio dextrinosolvens showed the strongest connection to faster aging — meaning more of this microbe tended to track with a speedier aging pace.

3. What This Could Mean for Staying Healthy

The gut microbiome changes in response to what you eat, how you live, and even your environment. Unlike your fixed DNA, your microbial community can shift day by day. That makes it a particularly exciting clue for healthy aging — if certain microbes consistently link to slower aging, they may point toward dietary or lifestyle habits that could help.

It's worth noting that the study only shows a connection, not that taking probiotics will slow aging outright. But it's an encouraging starting point. B. adolescentis, for example, is already known to produce compounds like GABA and folate that support brain health and calm inflammation.

The researchers hope their work will inspire future studies to test whether deliberately nurturing friendly gut microbes can actually slow biological aging — a tantalizing possibility for healthy, independent living decades down the road.

Fun fact

Your gut is home to more microbes than there are stars in the Milky Way — roughly 150 times more bacterial cells than human cells in your body!
