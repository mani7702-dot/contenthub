How do olive oil and fish help tiny cell proteins keep your heart and brain healthy?

2026-10-263 min

Scientists discovered that people who eat a Mediterranean diet have higher levels of two tiny mitochondrial proteins — humanin and SHMOOSE — that help protect the heart and brain from aging damage.

Did you know your cells have tiny protein helpers — so small you need a super-powerful microscope to see them — that act like bodyguards for your heart and brain?

A new study found that eating a Mediterranean diet (think olive oil, fish, beans, fruits, and vegetables) helps your body make more of these microscopic protectors. And that might be one reason doctors always say this eating style is so good for you.

What are these tiny cell bodyguards?

Inside almost every cell in your body live tiny power plants called mitochondria. Scientists used to think mitochondria only made energy. But now we know they also produce miniature proteins that act like messengers — telling your cells how to stay healthy as you grow.

Two of these miniature proteins have superhero-sounding jobs:

1. Humanin — helps protect nerve cells in your brain and keeps blood vessels strong

2. SHMOOSE — may guard against brain problems and support cellular repair

How does food talk to your cells?

Researchers at the USC Leonard Davis School of Gerontology studied 49 older adults. Half followed the Mediterranean diet closely. The scientists measured how much of these tiny proteins were floating in each person's blood.

Here is what they found:

  • People who ate more olive oil, fish, and legumes had higher levels of humanin.
  • People who used olive oil and ate fewer refined carbs (like white bread and sugary snacks) had higher levels of SHMOOSE.
  • Both groups with high protein levels also showed lower signs of cellular stress — that is, fewer harmful molecules that can damage DNA and cause aging.

It is almost like your food sends text messages to your mitochondria, and those messages say: "Build more bodyguards today!"

A real-world recipe for tiny protectors

The Mediterranean diet is not a strict rulebook — it is a way of eating that families have enjoyed for thousands of years around the Mediterranean Sea (that is the region bordering southern Europe, northern Africa, and the Middle East). Here is how to invite these protective proteins to dinner:

  • Drizzle olive oil on vegetables, bread, or salads instead of butter
  • Eat fish like salmon, sardines, or mackerel a couple of times a week
  • Add beans and lentils to soups, stews, or salads
  • Choose whole fruits and vegetables in every color of the rainbow
  • Skip ultra-processed snacks — the ones with a long list of unpronounceable ingredients

Try this at home

Ask a grown-up to help you make a simple "rainbow plate": pick at least one food from each color of the rainbow (red tomatoes, orange carrots, yellow peppers, green beans, blue/purple grapes). That single plate gives your cells vitamins, fiber, and the building blocks for those protective proteins!

Fun Fact: The humanin protein was first discovered by scientists over 20 years ago. Today, researchers think it might not only protect the brain but also help people live longer, healthier lives — all from eating real, fresh foods.
