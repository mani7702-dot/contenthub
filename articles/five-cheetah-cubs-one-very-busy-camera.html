Five cheetah cubs, one very busy camera

2026-10-112 min

Five cheetah cubs were born at the Smithsonian's conservation park — and you can watch all of them, around the clock, on a livestream.

Five cheetah cubs were born on October 5th at the Smithsonian National Zoo and Conservation Biology Institute in Virginia. Their mother is a cheetah named Zura.

The cubs are the fourth generation in a bloodline that started at the institute with their great-grandmother, a cheetah called Sanurra. Cheetahs are the fastest land animals on Earth — they can reach around 110 kilometres per hour in short bursts — but raising cubs is slow, careful work.

The camera that never sleeps

You cannot visit the cubs in person yet, but you do not have to wait. The Smithsonian runs the Cheetah Cub Cam, a 24-hour livestream of the den where the cubs sleep, wobble, and slowly work out how to be cheetahs.

Expect a lot of sleeping, a lot of tumbling, and one very patient mother.

Why breeding programmes matter

Cheetahs are listed as vulnerable — there are far fewer of them in the wild than there used to be. Every healthy cub in a breeding programme is a small insurance policy for the species.

The cubs should be on the camera for about six to seven months, until they decide they would rather sleep somewhere else.
