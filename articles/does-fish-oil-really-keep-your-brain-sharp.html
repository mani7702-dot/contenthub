Does fish oil really keep your brain sharp?

2026-10-243 min

A careful two-year study found that omega-3 fish oil pills did reach the brain, but they did not boost memory or protect thinking skills in older adults — suggesting food-first nutrition may beat supplements.

1. Does fish oil really keep your brain sharp?

The supplement aisle test

Walk down almost any grocery aisle and you will spot bright bottles promising that fish oil can protect your memory. Millions of people pop these omega-3 pills every day, hoping to keep their brains strong as they grow older. So does the science actually back it up? A brand-new, careful study set out to find out.

What the two-year study checked

Researchers at Keck Medicine of USC ran a careful study with older adults. For two full years, one group took a high-dose omega-3 (specifically DHA) supplement every day, while another group took a placebo that looked and tasted the same. Neither the volunteers nor the scientists knew who got which pill — that is called a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, the gold standard for testing medicines and supplements.

The surprising result

Here is the twist: the omega-3s did reach the brain. Brain scans showed the nutrients actually arrived where they were supposed to go. But after two years of daily pills, the people taking fish oil did not score better on memory tests and thinking tests. Their results looked just like the placebo group. As the lead doctor said, the pills delivered the oil but not the promised brain boost.

What this means (simply)

Omega-3s are still important — they help brain cells talk to each other and stay healthy. But the study suggests that getting them from a bottle may not be a magic helper against memory lapses. Getting these nutrients from real foods, like salmon, sardines, walnuts, and flax seeds, along with an overall balanced diet, may be the smarter move.

Try this at home

Ask a grown-up to help you make a simple snack: toast spread with a little olive oil, topped with walnut pieces and a sprinkle of chia seeds. That combo gives your brain some of the same healthy fats found in fish oil — but in a form your body may use more easily.

Fun Fact

The human brain is about 60% fat, which is why nutrients like omega-3s matter so much. Yet this study reminds us that the brain works best when those fats come packaged inside real foods, not just a single pill.

for-grownups: A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial from Keck Medicine of USC (Yassine et al., eBioMedicine, DOI 10.1016/j.ebiom.2026.106316) gave older adults at risk for dementia a high-dose DHA (omega-3) supplement daily for two years. Brain imaging confirmed the omega-3s reached the central nervous system (target engagement was achieved), yet cognitive test scores, memory, and hippocampal volume did not differ significantly from placebo. The authors note DHA remains an essential nutrient for brain cell connections, but the results do not support high-dose fish-oil supplements as a standalone Alzheimer's prevention strategy in this at-risk group. They recommend obtaining omega-3s through a balanced diet (e.g., fatty fish, walnuts, flax) as part of an overall healthy lifestyle.
