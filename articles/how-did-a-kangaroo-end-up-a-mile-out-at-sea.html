How Did a Kangaroo End Up a Mile Out at Sea?

2026-10-283 min

A scuba diver and his crew in South Australia rescued a kangaroo over a mile from shore. Spotting the exhausted joey floating in the calm sea, they scooped him up and brought him safely back to land.

A scuba diver and his crew in South Australia rescued a kangaroo over a mile from shore. Spotting the exhausted joey floating in the calm sea, they scooped him up and brought him safely back to land.

The Unexpected Passenger

It was an ordinary dive in Boston Bay, South Australia, when the boat's captain spotted something unusual floating in the water. At first, he thought it might be a piece of driftwood or a stray buoy. But as they drew closer, the truth became clear: a kangaroo was treading water over a mile from dry land.

Jake Murray, who was both a scuba diver and a farmer, helped lead the rescue. "He must have had a big night and one of the boys put a dare on and he's gone for a splash and put himself into mischief!" Murray joked with ABC News Australia, though he made sure his grip was gentle when securing the kangaroo's powerful legs.

The juvenile roo was clearly exhausted, dosing off in Murray's arms even as the boat made its way back toward shore. The calm sea and the vessel's lowered water door made the pickup surprisingly easy — the kangaroo seemed to recognize that the humans meant no harm.

From Sea to Safety

After the rescue, the kangaroo was assessed by local wildlife authorities. Kangaroos are excellent swimmers and occasionally venture into the ocean, especially along Australia's coastal regions. They can swim for long distances using their powerful tails as a rudder and their front paws for paddling. However, a young kangaroo can quickly become tired if separated from land for too long.

In 2022, ABC News reported a similar incident where a 17-year-old rookie lifeguard in New South Wales jumped into action to save a kangaroo that had been pushed into the sea by powerful waves while scampering along large rocks. These stories remind us that while kangaroos are great swimmers, they're always safer close to shore.

Onboard the rescue boat, the kangaroo was kept calm and hydrated. After a short rest period, the team guided him back into the shallows near the beach. Within minutes, he was hopping away under his own strength, leaving the crew with a story too "Australiana" to believe.

Why This Matters

Kangaroos are marsupials native to Australia, with over 60 species ranging in size from the tiny daisy-bounce potoroo to the enormous red kangaroo (the largest marsupial in the world). While they're built for hopping across dry land, they're also capable swimmers — though they rarely find themselves in situations where they need to be rescued at sea.

These unusual rescue stories highlight the importance of coastal safety for all wildlife. Whether it's a kangaroo swept out to sea by strong waves or a walrus calf separated from its herd, animal rescues bring communities together and remind us that help can come from the most unexpected places.

Fun Fact

A baby kangaroo is called a joey — just like a baby walrus is called a calf! And while you might think a creature as large as a kangaroo would sink, they actually float quite well thanks to their lean muscle mass and buoyant tails. Kangaroos also can't walk backwards, which is why they've evolved hopping as their primary mode of travel.
