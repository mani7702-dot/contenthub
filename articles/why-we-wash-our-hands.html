Why we wash our hands

2026-10-143 min

Germs are too small to see, but you can still wash them away. Here is what soap actually does — and why twenty seconds matters.

Some things are too small to see. Germs are like that — tiny living things that can make you sick, riding around on your hands all day.

Water alone is not enough

Germs like to sit in a thin oily layer on your skin. Water slips right off oil, so rinsing your hands does very little on its own.

Soap is the trick

Soap is made of tiny molecules with two different ends. One end loves water, the other loves oil — so soap grabs the oily layer, pries the germs loose, and the water carries them down the drain.

Why twenty seconds

It takes time for the soap to work around every finger, thumb and nail. Twenty seconds is about as long as singing "happy birthday" twice — long enough for the germs to lose their grip.
