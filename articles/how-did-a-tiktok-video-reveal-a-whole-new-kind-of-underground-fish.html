How Did a TikTok Video Reveal a Whole New Kind of Underground Fish?

2026-10-29T14:30:00+05:302 min

A pale, translucent fish with skin-covered eyes was hiding in plain sight — spotted not on a research expedition, but in a TikTok video showing it emerging from a borewell in India.

How Did a TikTok Video Reveal a Whole New Kind of Underground Fish?

Imagine scrolling through your phone and spotting an animal no scientist has ever seen before.

That's almost what happened in 2024, when a video posted to TikTok showed a tiny, ghostly fish emerging from a borewell near the India–Nepal border. The creature looked ordinary to most viewers — a quick clip of something pale wriggling in the water — but to the researchers who later watched it, the fish in the video was extraordinary. It had no pigment, its eyes were barely visible under a thin layer of skin, and it moved with a quiet purpose that screamed deep-darkness specialist.

The fish was later named Gangaichthys indonepalicus — a brand-new species described by science in 2026. But its journey from TikTok clip to scientific paper began with a question: What on Earth is that?

A Video, A Hunch, and a Hunt

The TikTok video was shot near a village in Bihar, India, showing a small, almost see-through fish swimming up through the calm water of a borewell — a narrow shaft drilled into the ground to reach underground aquifers. The person filming had probably just thought it was an unusual sight.

But co-author Akshay Khandekar of the Thackeray Wildlife Foundation recognized something special. The fish's unusual features — complete lack of body color, tiny eyes covered by skin, and a long, eel-like body — matched no known species from the region. "The unique morphology of the fish in the video, and the fact that there are no known stygobitic [underground] fish from the area, motivated us to track down the species," he explained.

Armed with this hunch, the team — which included scientists from the Zoological Survey of India and the Shanghai Synchrotron Radiation Facility — set out across Bihar. Finding willing well owners took patience, and extracting water from the right borewells required thousands of liters of pumping. After one particularly patient effort, they finally pulled up the first real specimen: a 4 cm (about 1.5 inch) long creature that looked like something from another planet.

Life in the Dark

What makes Gangaichthys indonepalicus so special is that it is one of the few known fish that lives entirely underground. Its ancestors likely entered the aquifers below the Indo-Gangetic Plain long ago, evolving slowly for thousands of years without ever seeing sunlight. In the darkness, natural selection stripped away the features animals usually need at the surface: eyes became tiny and were covered by skin, pigment cells disappeared entirely, and even ribs vanished.

Scientists used high-resolution CT scans and genetic analysis to confirm that the fish belongs to a family of earthworm eels (Chaudhuriidae), and it is the very first member of that family known to live this way. The formal description was published in the journal ZooKeys, cementing its place not just as a new species, but as a new genus — a completely new branch on the tree of life.

Fun Fact

The same TikTok video that launched a scientific discovery also captured the fish in its natural habitat. After genetic analysis confirmed the borewell population matched the video, researchers realized that ordinary people — well, ordinary videographers — may have been the first humans to document a brand-new vertebrate species on the planet.

What other secrets are hiding in the videos on your phone? All it takes is one curious biologist watching at the right time.
