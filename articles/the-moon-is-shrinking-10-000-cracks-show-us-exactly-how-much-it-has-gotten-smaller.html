The moon is shrinking — 10,000 cracks show us exactly how much it has gotten smaller

2026-10-243 min

A new study found over 10,000 fault scarps on the moon's surface — cracks that form when the moon's interior cools and its rocky shell contracts. The data shows our nearest celestial neighbor has shrunk by about 148 feet over the past 400 million years.

Why is the moon slowly shrinking — and what do 10,000 cracks tell us?

The shrinking neighbor

Look up at the moon on a clear night and you might think it looks perfectly still and unchanged. But scientists have discovered something surprising: our nearest celestial neighbor is slowly, slowly shrinking. And they have spotted 10,000 cracks across its surface that act like a record of how much it has gotten smaller.

How a giant baby became a smaller adult

The moon is thought to have formed about 4.5 billion years ago when a Mars-sized object slammed into our young Earth. The debris from that violent collision eventually clumped together to form the moon — a molten, glowing ball of rock. As it has sat in space ever since, the moon has been cooling down. When hot rock cools, it contracts, or gets smaller. Over billions of years, the moon's rocky outer shell has slowly squeezed inward.

10,000 cracks that write the story

Dr. Thomas Watters and his team studied images taken by NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera (LROC) and found more than 10,000 fault scarps — long, curved cracks where the moon's surface has been pushed upward. These cracks form because the moon's single rocky shell is breaking as it shrinks. Unlike Earth, the moon has no tectonic plates, so these fractures appear all across its surface instead of at plate boundaries. Based on these cracks, scientists estimate that the moon's radius has shrunk by about 148 feet (45 meters) over the past 400 to 800 million years. That is roughly the height of a 45-story building!

The moon still has a few tricks left

Even though the moon is small and seems quiet, it is not completely dead. As the moon keeps cooling and shrinking a little bit more each year, scientists expect new cracks to form. The moon also experiences weak "moonquakes" — tremors caused by tidal stress from Earth's gravity. These moonquakes can last up to a full hour, much longer than earthquakes on Earth, because the moon's dry, broken crust lets seismic waves bounce around inside.

Try this at home

Draw a big circle on a balloon to represent the moon. Then let the air out slowly — watch how the circle wrinkles and folds as the surface area shrinks. That is a tiny model of what is happening to the real moon as it cools in space!

Fun Fact

The moon is still shrinking today — but so slowly that it would take a human lifetime just to see a few millimeters of change. The 10,000+ fault scarps found by NASA's LRO were formed over hundreds of millions of years, and new ones are still being born!

---

For grown-ups: The study was led by Dr. Thomas Watters (Smithsonian's National Air and Space Museum) and published on October 14, 2026, in The Planetary Science Journal. The team analyzed LROC images to map fault scarps across the moon, revealing ongoing contraction. NASA first reported the shrinking moon in 2010. Wikipedia confirms the moon has an iron-rich core (~240 km radius) and a partially molten boundary layer; moonquakes from tidal stress can last up to an hour due to the moon's dry, fragmented crust.
