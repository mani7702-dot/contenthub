Can a 15-minute water trick fix carpal tunnel without surgery?

2026-10-263 min

A simple injection that uses fluid to free a squeezed nerve may help people avoid carpal tunnel surgery — and it only takes about 15 minutes.

Can a 15-minute water trick fix carpal tunnel without surgery?

When your hand goes to sleep (and won't wake up)

You have been typing, drawing, or playing an instrument for hours, and now your fingers feel numb and tingly. That annoying "pins and needles" feeling could be carpal tunnel syndrome — when a nerve gets squished inside a tiny tunnel in your wrist. It affects roughly 5 to 10 percent of adults, and it can make everyday tasks like writing or opening doors surprisingly hard.

Why fingers go numb

Inside your wrist runs a narrow bony canal called the carpal tunnel. The median nerve travels through this tight space alongside tendons that bend your fingers. When the tunnel gets crowded — from swelling, repetitive motion, or just how some people are built — that nerve gets pressed and starts misfiring.

That is what causes the classic burning or tingling feeling, often waking people up at night. Some folks also feel weakness in their grip.

The water trick that frees the nerve

Doctors tested an alternative called nerve hydrodissection. Instead of injecting steroids (which can weaken tissue over time), they use real-time ultrasound to guide a thin needle and release a small amount of ordinary saline — basically saltwater — right around the squeezed nerve.

The fluid acts like a tiny cushion, gently separating the nerve from the sticky tissues that hold it down. It is a bit like slipping a dry noodle out of a tight jar by letting a little water loosen things up.

Longer relief, fewer risks

In a clinical trial with 63 patients, those who got the saline hydrodissection improved more and stayed better longer than people who only got traditional steroid shots. Since there are no steroids involved, there is less risk of skin thinning or tendon problems.

The whole procedure takes about 15 minutes and is done as an outpatient visit, meaning most people can go home the same day. Researchers say it could help some people delay or even avoid surgery altogether.

Try this at home

Wearing a wrist splint at night can take pressure off the nerve while you sleep. Taking short breaks every 30 to 45 minutes when you are doing repetitive hand work — like typing or drawing — gives your wrists a chance to rest. If symptoms keep coming back, talking to a grown-up about seeing a doctor is always smart.

Fun Fact

The words "carpal tunnel" come from the Latin word for "fruit" and the concept of a narrow passage — because the wrist bones form a little "fruit-stone" shaped tunnel. Ancient physicians described it over 2,000 years ago!
