How do corals act as underwater pollution detectives?

2026-10-273 min

Scientists discovered that corals store a chemical record of pollution in their tissues, and reefs with healthier 'chemical fingerprints' survived a devastating marine heat wave better. What does this teach us about protecting these underwater cities?

1. How do corals act as underwater pollution detectives?

The reef's hidden health diary

Corals might look like colorful rocks swaying in the ocean current, but scientists now know they are also living record-keepers. A new study found that coral tissues store a chemical diary of every pollutant that flows past them — from sunscreen chemicals to farm waste to medicine leftovers rinsed down rivers.

By reading this diary with a technique called metabolomics (the study of tiny molecules in living things), researchers discovered that reefs with cleaner "chemical fingerprints" bounced back stronger from one of the worst coral disasters on record.

What the corals told the scientists

In 2018, a research team led by Dr. Zachary Quinlan from the University of Hawai'i collected 380 coral samples from 16 reef sites along 70 miles of Maui's coastline. They studied two kinds of coral: lobe coral and rice coral.

Using metabolomics, they read the small molecules stored in each coral's tissues. The corals' chemical profiles naturally grouped into three distinct clusters — and those clusters matched perfectly with what the scientists knew about how much pollution each reef site received from land.

"We propose that this may be a way that you can actually evaluate how much impact humans are having on different sites," explained Dr. Quinlan.

The heat wave survival test

Here is the amazing part: the researchers then checked which corals had survived the 2015-2016 marine heat wave — the hottest ocean temperatures ever recorded at that time, which killed or bleached corals across the Pacific.

Reefs with healthier metabolomes (the ones with fewer pollutants stored up) had kept more coral cover during the heat wave. In other words, corals that were not burdened by constant land-based pollution were tougher when the ocean suddenly got too hot.

"If there's a site that has a lot of contamination, they can't move away and make a new home," said Dr. Quinlan. "They're just stuck there." But when pollution is reduced, these underwater cities become resilient.

Try this at home

Build a mini coral reef in a jar — fill a clear jar with clean sand, small stones, and dechlorinated water. Add a few drops of sea salt. This simple project teaches you how delicate coral ecosystems are and why keeping our oceans clean matters. Always use tap water conditioner for pets, never soap or toothpaste!

Fun Fact

Corals are not plants or rocks — they are tiny animals called polyps that live in colonies. Each polyp is only a few millimeters wide, but together they build enormous reef structures that are home to 25% of all marine life — even though reefs cover less than 1% of the ocean floor. Some corals are thousands of years old, making them older than the pyramids!
