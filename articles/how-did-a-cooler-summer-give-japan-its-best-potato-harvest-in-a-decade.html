How Did a Cooler Summer Give Japan Its Best Potato Harvest in a Decade?

2026-10-313 min

While heat and drought shrank harvests across much of the Northern Hemisphere, one cool, rainy island in northern Japan pulled off its finest potato crop in ten years — a reminder of how much good farming depends on the weather.

How Did a Cooler Summer Give Japan Its Best Potato Harvest in a Decade?

1. The Island That Beat the Heat

While scorching summers and long droughts shrivelled crops across southern Europe and beyond, one corner of the Northern Hemisphere quietly had its best growing season in years. Japan's northern island of Hokkaido — a cool, green place famous for its dairy farms and snowy winters — produced what industry experts are calling its finest potato harvest in a decade.

It's a turnaround worth noticing. Farming has grown more unpredictable almost everywhere as the climate shifts, yet Hokkaido shows that when the right conditions line up, nature can still deliver a banner year.

2. What Went Right This Time

The secret was a lucky mix of temperatures and rain. Last year, unusually high heat and patchy rainfall thinned out the crop and hurt the quality of the potatoes. This year, Hokkaido stayed noticeably cooler. Its capital, Sapporo, recorded only four days above 86°F (30°C), compared with sixteen such days the year before.

Cooler air helped in several ways. The potatoes grew denser and rounder, the soil stayed firm enough for harvesting machinery to roll across without sinking, and the tubers kept better in storage for longer. Just as important, the island dodged the torrential downpours that can spread pests and leave fields too muddy to work.

3. Why One Island Matters So Much

Nicknamed the "potato typhoon" by industry watchers, the surge is a big deal because Hokkaido grows about 80% of all the potatoes in Japan. Major manufacturer Calbee expects the crop to reach roughly 240,000–250,000 tons — back to the company's usual, comfortable levels after a difficult 2025.

That's good news for everyone who loves French fries, potato chips, croquettes, and warm winter stews. A reliable harvest keeps prices steadier and shelves stocked.

4. A Lesson in Patience

Hokkaido's story isn't just about luck. Growers have been planting smarter, testing field-by-field fertilisation and extra irrigation where water is available. Still, weather remains the great variable no farmer can control — and this year, nature played along.

Fun Fact

The potato was the first vegetable ever grown in space. In 1995, NASA astronauts grew potatoes aboard the Space Shuttle Columbia to study how plants cope in orbit. Today, potatoes are the world's fourth-largest food crop, after rice, wheat, and maize — and the most popular non-cereal crop on Earth.
